How to teach your teenager about the stock market safely

Start with what your teenager already knows: the brands they use. Explain that a share is a small piece of one of those businesses, then let them practise with virtual money so that a mistake costs nothing. Under 18 they can't deal with a broker themselves, so practice is often the best first step anyway.

When can a teenager start learning about shares?

Whenever they are curious. There is no right age to learn, and the law only limits what they can do, not what they can understand. A good moment is when they ask why a brand's price is in the news, or what a relative means by "the market". The first conversation doesn't need any numbers.

How do you explain a share with everyday brands?

Use something they already use. Choose a company behind a phone network, a bank or a snack they like, and ask who owns it. Explain that a share is a very small piece of that company, that lots of people own those pieces, and that the price changes as people change their minds about how the business will do.

Then ask them how they think it makes money. If they can answer that, they have learned the most useful question in investing.

Keep it about the business, not about the price, at least at first, because a teenager who only watches a price learns to feel excited, and one who follows a business learns to think. You can extend this by comparing two companies in the same line of work, and asking why one might be doing better. You don't need to know the answers. Finding out together is half the value, and it shows them that nobody has all the answers, including the people on the news.

How do you talk about risk and losses?

Treat losses as information. When a practice trade goes wrong, ask what they expected and what happened, and don't rush to fix it. The panic-selling guide is a good one to read together.

Be honest that markets can fall a long way and stay down. Teach them to be suspicious of anyone promising sure returns, and that SEBI tells people not to rely on hot tips, including those in group chats.

What does the law allow for under-18s?

SEBI's FAQ says a demat account can be opened in a minor's name and operated by a guardian, but that a minor can't enter into a contract with a stock broker to buy or sell any security. The guide for teenagers has the details. Mutual fund units can be held with a guardian as well.
