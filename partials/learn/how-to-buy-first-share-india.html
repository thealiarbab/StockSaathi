How to buy your first share in India, step by step

To buy your first share in India you need three accounts: a bank account, a trading account with a SEBI-registered broker, and a demat account. You complete KYC, add money, place an order on NSE or BSE, and the shares reach your demat account after settlement. Under 18, the rules are different.

Key takeaways

  • SEBI says you need three accounts to invest in shares: bank, trading and demat.
  • KYC is compulsory under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, and you can complete it online with e-KYC.
  • Buy only through a SEBI-registered broker, and check the broker on SEBI's or the exchange's website.
  • Under 18 you can't enter a contract with a stock broker to buy shares, so the steps here apply from age 18.
  • Note every charge before you begin, because charges differ between brokers.

How do you open the accounts?

First, pick a SEBI-registered broker. StockSaathi doesn't recommend any, and you can check a broker's registration on SEBI's website or on an exchange's website. You fill in an account opening form and submit KYC, or Know Your Client, documents, which SEBI's booklet says are mandatory under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002. These are officially valid documents proving your identity and address, such as a PAN card, Aadhaar, passport, voter ID or driving licence.

Many people now do this online through e-KYC, which SEBI's booklet describes as filling in the form on the broker's website, uploading scanned documents, completing in-person verification over a video call and signing digitally. Before you sign anything, SEBI's advice is simple: read the documents, don't sign blanks, and register your own mobile number and email so that alerts reach you.

Two details are worth checking. SEBI's booklet says a Power of Attorney is optional and voluntary, so don't treat it as compulsory. And a Basic Services Demat Account may suit a small investor: under SEBI's circular of June 2024 it is for holdings up to ₹10 lakh, with no annual maintenance charge up to ₹4 lakh.

How do you place your first order?

Log in to the broker's app or website, search for the company, and choose how many shares you want. You will be asked for an order type. A market order buys at the best price available, and a limit order buys only at your price or better.

The guide to order types explains both, along with orders placed after hours. In the normal market, NSE trades from 9:15 am to 3:30 pm on trading days, and SEBI's booklet notes that exchanges don't trade on Saturdays, Sundays or declared holidays.

You can buy a single share, so the smallest order is usually just the price of one share plus charges. When the order goes through, you will receive a contract note, which SEBI's booklet describes as evidence of the trade with details such as the price, time and brokerage. Keep it, and check that it matches what you meant to do.

If it doesn't, contact the broker straight away. Your first order is a good moment to go slowly, because almost every early mistake here comes from rushing.

What happens after you buy?

The trade settles the next working day, under India's T+1 settlement cycle, and the shares arrive in your demat account. If you later sell, the money comes back through the same route. You will also get account statements from your broker and depository, which are worth reading so that you notice anything unusual.

What charges apply?

They vary, so SEBI's booklet tells you to carefully note all charges, fees and brokerage on your accounts and keep a record. On a very small purchase, even a modest charge can be a large share of what you spent, which the guide on how much money you need to start looks at.

Can you do this if you are under 18?

No. SEBI's FAQ says a minor can't enter into a contract with a stock broker to buy or sell securities. A minor can have a demat account operated by a guardian, as the guide for teenagers explains. Students aged 18 or over can follow the steps above.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to buy my first share?

As little as the price of one share plus charges. Many listed shares cost a few hundred rupees, though charges matter more on small orders.

Can I buy shares without a demat account?

Not for normal share buying. SEBI's booklet says you need a bank account, a trading account and a demat account to invest in equity shares.

How long does it take for shares to appear in my account?

Trades settle on the next working day under T+1, so shares reach your demat account after that.
