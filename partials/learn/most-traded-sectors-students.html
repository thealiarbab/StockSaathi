The sectors Indian students trade most

Students on StockSaathi trade big, familiar sectors most. Energy leads with 17.3% of all practice trades, then banking at 14.9% and IT services at 10.4%, so three sectors make up 42.6% of trades. That says more about which names beginners know than about which sectors are good investments.

Why do beginners choose familiar sectors?

Because they trade what they already know. Energy is at the top mainly because of one company, Reliance Industries, which is also the most common first buy on StockSaathi. Banks and IT firms are household names too: their brands are everywhere, and they appear in the news and at family dinner tables. When you have no other way to choose, a name you recognise feels like the safe option. That is a natural place to start learning, because it is easier to follow a business whose products you use.

The risk is that a list of famous names can quietly become a bet on just two or three lines of business. Across all trades, 56.8% are in Nifty 50 companies, so most activity sits in the largest companies in the market. A portfolio that looks varied because it holds six big brands may depend on far fewer things than it seems to. That gap between how a portfolio looks and how it behaves is worth checking.

What is a sector?

A sector is a group of companies in the same broad line of business, such as banks or software firms. Sectors matter because companies in one sector often rise and fall together, since the same things affect them: interest rates for banks, the rupee and overseas demand for IT exporters, oil prices for energy. Different data providers draw these lines differently, so a company can sit in one sector on one site and another elsewhere.

Does trading one sector raise your risk?

Yes. Holding five banks is closer to one big bet than to five separate ones, because a shock to banking hits all of them at once. SEBI's investor booklet describes unsystematic risk as risk tied to a company or industry, and asset allocation across companies and asset classes as one way to reduce risk.

Which sectors do students trade least?

Everything outside the top few gets a small slice each. The top five sectors take about 54% of all trades, and no sector below them reaches 4%. Those smaller groups include businesses that many students rarely hear about, even though they are large parts of the economy.

A sector being quiet on StockSaathi only means beginners aren't looking there, and it says nothing about how those companies are doing. A useful exercise is to pick a sector you have never traded and find out how its companies earn money.

Do these habits change with experience?

This table can't show that, because it counts all trades together. What StockSaathi's data does show is that beginners move past the simplest order type quickly: 86.4% of people who trade have used a limit order or an after-market order. You can read about those in the guide to order types. Choosing what to buy is a skill too, and it improves the same way, by making a choice, writing down why and checking later.

How can you spread a practice portfolio across sectors?

Start by listing what you own by sector, not by name. Ask what single piece of news would hurt most of it at once. Try adding a business that depends on something different, or look at a fund or ETF, which holds many sectors in one purchase.

You can also look beyond the largest companies: the guide to company sizes explains how they differ. This is about understanding your own exposure, and it isn't a suggestion to buy any sector.
