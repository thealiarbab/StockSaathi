What is a demat account?

A demat account holds your shares and other securities in electronic form, in the way a bank account holds money. You open one through a Depository Participant of NSDL or CDSL. It keeps shares, while a separate trading account places your orders, and you need both to buy shares in India.

Key takeaways

  • A demat account holds securities in dematerialised, or electronic, form.
  • India has 2 depositories, NSDL and CDSL, and you open an account through a Depository Participant.
  • A trading account places orders, and a demat account stores what you bought.
  • A Basic Services Demat Account covers holdings up to ₹10 lakh, with no annual charge up to ₹4 lakh and at most ₹100 from there to ₹10 lakh.

What is a demat account in simple words?

Demat is short for dematerialised, which means turned from paper into electronic form. Shares used to be paper certificates. Now they are entries in an account, so a demat account is to shares roughly what a bank account is to money: when you buy, your holding goes up, and when you sell, it goes down. SEBI's financial education booklet describes it as an account with a depository participant for holding securities in electronic form.

The Depositories Act, 1996 provides for this electronic maintenance and transfer of ownership, as SEBI's booklet notes. There is no certificate to lose, and the record of what you own sits with the depository. That is the reason a demat account matters even to someone who never thinks about it: it is the actual record that you own the shares. Your broker's app shows you a view of your holdings, but the demat account is where they are held.

This is also why statements from your depository are worth reading. They come from a different source than your broker, so a mismatch between the two is something you would want to spot early, and SEBI's booklet tells investors to check them. Registering your own mobile number and email with your depository participant means alerts about activity in the account come straight to you, rather than depending on anyone else.

Who are the depository and the DP?

A depository is the institution that holds securities electronically. India's two are National Securities Depository Limited (NSDL) and Central Depository Services (India) Limited (CDSL). You don't deal with them directly. You open your account through a Depository Participant, or DP, a SEBI-registered agent of the depository, which is often a broker or a bank.

What do you need to open one?

KYC documents, which SEBI's booklet says are mandatory, covering proof of identity and proof of address, such as a PAN card or Aadhaar. Many providers let you do this online through e-KYC. The step-by-step guide to buying a share covers the order of things, and the guide for students covers age.

What charges does a demat account have?

Charges differ between providers, and many charge an annual maintenance fee. If you are a small investor, a Basic Services Demat Account, or BSDA, can reduce that. Under SEBI's circular of 28 June 2024, the value of securities held can't exceed ₹10 lakh at any point.

There is no annual charge up to ₹4 lakh, and at most ₹100 between ₹4 lakh and ₹10 lakh. One person can have one such account. Older material quotes lower limits, so check the date of anything you read.

Can a minor have a demat account?

Yes, operated by a guardian, but a minor can't trade through a broker in the normal way. The guide for teenagers sets out what SEBI allows.

How does a demat account help when you sell?

When you sell shares, they leave your demat account and go to the buyer, and the money reaches you after settlement. Under India's T+1 settlement cycle that happens on the next working day. You don't have to post or sign anything, which is a long way from the days of paper certificates, when lost or forged documents were a real problem.

Frequently asked questions

Is a demat account the same as a trading account?

No. A demat account holds your shares, while a trading account places your orders. You need both to buy and sell shares.

What is the full form of demat?

Demat is short for dematerialised, meaning held in electronic form rather than as paper certificates.

Is there a charge for a demat account?

Often an annual maintenance charge, which varies by provider. A Basic Services Demat Account has no annual charge up to ₹4 lakh of holdings, under SEBI's 2024 circular.

Demat account kya hota hai?
