Overtrading and churning: when more trades means worse results

Overtrading means buying and selling far more often than any plan calls for, usually out of boredom, excitement or the urge to fix a loss. Each trade has a cost, and being out of the market at the wrong moment has a bigger one. ₹1,00,000 in the Nifty 50 without its 10 best days ended at ₹2,29,254, not ₹3,75,794.

How much does missing the best days cost?

More than most people expect. The table takes ₹1,00,000 in the Nifty 50 from 3 January 2011 to 30 October 2026, then removes the index's biggest one-day gains from the record.

What is overtrading?

Overtrading is trading more often than your reasons justify. Churning is a close relative: going in and out of the same stock again and again within days. Neither is defined by a magic number, since a professional and a beginner have very different reasons to trade. A useful test is whether you could explain each trade to someone a week later.

How do trades cost you money?

Three ways. Each trade has direct charges, which SEBI's booklet tells investors to note down before they start. Each one also has a small hidden cost in the gap between buying and selling prices, which matters more in thinly traded shares. The third cost is usually the biggest: time out of the market.

Trading in and out means you aren't holding on some of the days that decide the result, and the best days tend to arrive when nerves are worst. In this data, 6 of the 10 best days came within 10 sessions of one of the 10 worst, such as the gain of 8.76% on 7 April 2020, two weeks after the fall of 13.0% on 23 March. Someone who sold in the panic and planned to come back later would very likely have missed it.

What did SEBI find about frequent derivatives trading?

The most active kind of trading has the most striking record. In its press release of 23 September 2024, SEBI reported that 93% of individual traders in equity futures and options lost money over the three years from FY22 to FY24, with average losses of around ₹2 lakh per trader including transaction costs. Its later study, published in August 2026, put the share of loss-making individuals at 87.7% in FY26, down from 90.9% in FY25.

Derivatives are a very different product from simply buying shares, so these numbers don't describe an ordinary investor. They are still a reminder that doing more isn't the same as earning more.

What are the warning signs?

You check the app many times an hour. You trade to feel something rather than because something changed. After a loss, you make a bigger trade to win it back.

You can't name the reason for last week's trades. And you feel restless on days when you haven't placed an order.

How does StockSaathi's coach spot it?

The coach flags overtrading on the 10th trade you place by hand in a day, then again at the 20th and 40th. It leaves your first day alone, since that is for trying things out, and it doesn't count orders the matcher filled later, because you decided those earlier. It flags churning on the sale that completes a 3rd quick round trip in one stock within 30 days, each held under 5 days. Both notes come after the trade, with your own numbers.

How can you set limits?

Write down the reason for a trade before placing it, and if you can't, don't place it. Decide how many trades you will make in a month, and review them at the end of it. Use a limit order and walk away, so you aren't watching the screen. If a loss makes you want to act straight away, the panic-selling guide is worth reading first.

Frequently asked questions

How many trades is too many?

There is no fixed number. A better test is whether each trade has a reason you can write down. StockSaathi's coach flags the 10th hand-placed trade in one day as a prompt to pause and look.

Why is overtrading bad for beginners?

Each trade has costs, and frequent trading raises the chance of being out of the market on the few days that decide most of the return.

What is the difference between overtrading and churning?

Overtrading is trading too much in general. Churning is going in and out of the same stock repeatedly within days.

Overtrading kya hota hai?
