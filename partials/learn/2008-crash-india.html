The 2008 crash in India: why selling early won, and why that's rare

In 2008 the Nifty 50 fell 59.9% from its January record to its October low. ₹1,00,000 that stayed invested shrank to ₹40,144, while someone who sold on day three kept ₹98,604. Selling early won that year, but only because the fall was unusually deep and long, and getting back in was the harder decision.

What happened to the Nifty 50 in 2008?

The Nifty 50 closed at a record 6,287.85 on 8 January 2008 and then fell for most of the year, ending its slide at 2,524.20 on 27 October 2008. The table follows ₹1,00,000 that moves exactly with the index, next to the same money sold at the close of day three and kept in cash.

Why did selling early win in 2008?

Selling won because the fall kept going for ten months and went very deep. On day three the Nifty was only 1.4% below its record, so the seller got out almost at the top. The index then lost 59.9%, and in the autumn the crisis spread through the global banking system: on 15 September 2008 the American bank Lehman Brothers filed what a US Congress hearing record calls the largest bankruptcy proceeding in that country's history. By the bottom in late October, the seller's cash was worth almost two and a half times the held portfolio.

That outcome depended on luck as much as judgement. The seller in this replay did not know a global crisis was coming. They sold because the market dipped a little in its first week, which is also how many beginners sell in falls that turn out to be short.

How long did the Nifty take to recover from the 2008 crash?

Almost three years from the record. The Nifty 50 first closed back at its 8 January 2008 level on 9 November 2010, 496 trading days, the days the exchange is open, after the October 2008 bottom. A year after the bottom the held portfolio had already climbed from ₹40,144 to ₹77,080, but it still sat below where it started.

That long wait is the other half of the story. Someone who sold in January 2008 and wanted back in had to choose a day to buy, and there was no sign telling them when. Buying back too late, after a big rebound, could undo much of the advantage of selling early.

Why is a 2008-style crash rare?

Since regaining its record in November 2010 the Nifty 50 has fallen 10% or more nine times, and none of those falls came close to 2008. The deepest, the COVID-19 crash of 2020, was 38.4%. Every one of the eight completed falls got back to its old high, with a median of about nine and a half months, as the record of every 10% Nifty fall since 2010 shows. In those falls, selling early and staying in cash would have meant missing a recovery that came within months, as it did in the COVID-19 crash.

So 2008 is the honest counter-example, not the rule. A crash replay is useful because it shows both sides: a case where holding won and a case where selling did. What made 2008 different was a crisis in the banking system itself that lasted most of a year, not the speed of the first few days. Nobody can tell in the first week of a fall which kind it will become, and StockSaathi can't tell you either.

Did the Sensex and Nifty fall by the same amount in 2008?

Almost. The Sensex closed at 20,873.33 on 8 January 2008 and at 8,451.01 on 20 November 2008, its lowest close that year, a fall of 59.5%. The Nifty 50 fell 59.9% from the same January day to its low on 27 October. The two indices hold many of the same large companies, so a crash of this size hits both.

What did a monthly SIP do through the 2008 crash?

It went deep into loss first, then finished well ahead. Put ₹5,000 into the Nifty 50 on the January 2008 high and again on the first session of each month, and ₹50,000 had gone in by 27 October 2008, worth ₹26,864, down 46.3%. By 9 November 2010, when the index regained its high, ₹1,75,000 had gone in and it was worth ₹2,53,454, up 44.8%.

That uses the index itself, so it leaves out fund costs and dividends. It also took nearly three years and a lot of nerve, because the statement looked terrible for months. Panic selling is what usually ends a plan like this one early.
