How long does the Nifty 50 take to recover from a 10%, 20% or 30% fall?

Since its record high of November 2010 the Nifty 50 has fallen 10% or more nine times. Eight of those falls recovered fully, taking a median of 200 trading days, about nine and a half months, to get back to the old high. Bigger falls took longer, and history describes what happened; it does not promise what will.

Key takeaways

  • Since its record high of November 2010 the Nifty 50 has fallen 10% or more 9 times; 8 of those falls have fully recovered and 1 is still open.
  • The median 10% fall took 200 trading days to climb back to its old high; the fastest took 73 and the slowest took 682.
  • Falls of 20% or more happened 3 times, reaching their lows in 2011, 2016 and 2020, and took a median of 265 trading days to recover from the day the fall reached 20%.
  • Only one of these falls went past 30%: the COVID-19 crash, when the Nifty dropped 38.4% and still regained its January 2020 high by November 2020.
  • At the end of October 2026 the Nifty was 12.4% below its January 2026 high, in a fall that has not yet recovered.

How long does a 10% fall in the Nifty usually last?

The typical 10% fall took about nine and a half months to undo. Across the eight falls that recovered, the median, the middle value when they are sorted, was 200 trading days. Half recovered faster and half slower.

The spread is wide. In 2019 the Nifty fell 11.4% between June and September and was back at its high by November, 73 trading days after the fall reached 10%. The fall from the November 2010 high took 682 trading days, more than two and a half years, because the index kept sliding for most of 2011 and then took almost two more years to get back. A short fall and a long one can look identical in their first few weeks, which is why the first weeks of a fall tell you very little.

What happened after the Nifty fell 20% or more?

Falls of 20% or more were rarer and slower. They happened three times: the 2010 to 2011 slide, the 2015 to 2016 fall and the COVID-19 crash of 2020. Counted from the day each fall reached 20%, they took a median of 265 trading days to get back to the old high, with the quickest, COVID-19, taking 165 and the slowest, the 2011 slide, taking 538.

A running high here means the highest close the index had reached so far. Measuring from it is strict: a fall only counts as recovered when the index closes at or above that exact level again, not when it simply bounces.

Has the Nifty fallen 30% since 2010?

Once. In the COVID-19 crash the Nifty 50 fell from 12,362.30 on 14 January 2020 to 7,610.25 on 23 March 2020, a drop of 38.4% in about two months. It was also one of the faster recoveries: the index closed back above its January high on 9 November 2020. From the day the fall first passed 30%, that took 161 trading days. Where did the Nifty stand at the end of October 2026?

In a fall that had not recovered. The Nifty 50 reached 26,328.55 on 2 January 2026, its highest close in the data. It first closed 10% below that on 12 March 2026, and on 30 October 2026 it closed at 23,053.10, 12.4% below the high. Its lowest close so far in this fall was 22,231.80 on 8 October 2026. As of the end of October the fall had lasted 157 trading days without recovering, against a median of 200 for past falls. Nobody knows whether it will end sooner or later than the ones before it.

Why does the recovery time of the index matter to a beginner?

The most expensive beginner mistake is selling in a panic near the bottom of a fall and locking in the loss. Knowing that the Nifty's past 10% falls took months, not days, to recover helps set expectations: a fall that is still going after a few weeks is normal for the index, not proof that something has broken. That is a statement about the index as a whole. The Nifty 50 is made of 50 large, liquid companies chosen by size, and its members change over time, so the index has recovered from every completed fall since 2010 while some individual stocks never returned to their old highs. A single stock can behave very differently from the index, which is why every stock page on StockSaathi lists that stock's own falls, including the ones that never recovered. Look at that record before deciding anything about one company, and remember that none of this predicts the next fall.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Nifty 50 always recover after a fall?

Every completed 10% fall in the Nifty 50 since its 2010 high has recovered, but the one that began in 2026 had not by the end of October 2026, and history is not a promise. The index's members also change over time as companies grow or shrink, so it can recover even when some of its former members never do.

How long did the Nifty take to recover from the COVID-19 crash?

From the day the fall first reached 10%, on 6 March 2020, to the first close back above the January 2020 high, on 9 November 2020, took 168 trading days. The lowest close was 7,610.25 on 23 March 2020.

What counts as a recovery in this guide?

A fall counts as recovered on the first day the Nifty 50 closes at or above the highest close it reached before the fall. Daily closing levels are used, not intraday prices, and dividends are not included.

Nifty 10% girne ke baad wapas upar aane mein kitna time lagta hai?

November 2010 ke high se ab tak Nifty 50 ke 10% ya usse zyada girne ke 8 recovered falls mein median 200 trading days laga, yaani lagbhag saadhe nau mahine. Sabse jaldi 73 din mein aur sabse der se 682 din mein recover hua. Ye itihaas hai, guarantee nahi.
