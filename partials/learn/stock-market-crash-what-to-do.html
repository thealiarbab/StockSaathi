The market just fell 10%. What does history say?

When the Nifty 50 falls 10%, history says it usually isn't over but isn't a disaster either. In nine such falls since its 2010 high, the index fell a median of 6.5 more points before turning. Three stopped before 15%, and three went past 20%. Nobody can tell you which kind you are in.

Key takeaways

  • The Nifty 50 has fallen 10% or more 9 times since its high of November 2010, and 8 of those falls have recovered.
  • After first reaching 10%, the median fall went 6.5 percentage points further before it hit its low.
  • 3 falls stopped before reaching 15%, and 3 went on to pass 20%.
  • The range was very wide: from 0.2 points further in 2018 to 28.4 points further in 2020.

Is this a correction or a crash?

You can't tell from inside it. A fall of 10% to 20% is usually called a correction, and anything beyond that a bear market or crash, but those labels are only applied afterwards. On the day a fall first reached 10% in March 2018 and in March 2020 the numbers looked the same.

The first was almost over. The second had 28.4 points still to go, and nobody knew which it was.

That is the honest answer to "is the market crashing?" A fall tells you what has happened, not what comes next, so anyone who claims certainty at this point is guessing. What history can do is set expectations. It says a further drop is normal, and it says a drop on this scale has happened several times in the last fifteen years without being the end of anything. It also says that some falls go much further than anyone expects, as the record of India's biggest crashes shows.

How long did the Nifty take to recover?

Months, not days. The full table of recovery times is in the Nifty recovery guide: the median was 200 trading days from the day the fall first reached 10%, with the fastest at 73 and the slowest at 682. All of that describes the index. A single share can behave very differently, and many stay below their old highs for years.

What do people usually regret?

The commonest regret is a decision made in the worst week. Selling in fear can turn a temporary fall into a permanent loss, and the panic-selling guide looks at how to spot it. The opposite regret exists too.

In the 2008 crash, people who sold early came out ahead for years, as the 2008 guide explains. So history doesn't give you a rule that holding is always right. What it gives you is a reason to slow down.

What can you do calmly while prices fall?

None of this is advice on whether to buy, hold or sell. These are ways to think clearly. Check why you own what you own, in writing. Ask whether anything changed at the company, or only the price.

Work out how much of your money sits in one place. Ignore tips: SEBI tells investors not to rely on them. Remember too that markets have their own emergency brakes: a fall of 10% in the index triggers a circuit breaker, a compulsory trading halt, under SEBI's rules.

Frequently asked questions

Is the stock market crashing?

No one can say during a fall. A 10% fall has happened 9 times in the Nifty 50 since 2010; some ended almost at once and some went past 20%. Labels like correction and crash are applied afterwards.

How often does the Nifty fall 10%?

9 times since its high of November 2010, or about once every couple of years, though they don't arrive at regular intervals.

Should I sell when the market falls 10%?

StockSaathi doesn't give advice. The record shows that selling in fear has often locked in losses, but in 2008 early sellers came out ahead, so no single rule fits every fall.
