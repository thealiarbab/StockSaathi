Mutual funds, stocks and ETFs explained for beginners

A stock is a small part of one company. A mutual fund pools many people's money and invests it for them, and you buy units at that day's NAV. An ETF is a basket of investments that trades on the exchange through the day, like a share. Beginners mostly need to know what they own and how they buy it.

Key takeaways

  • A stock is a share of one company, and its price moves through the trading day.
  • A mutual fund issues units at its NAV, published after the market closes.
  • An ETF is a basket of securities bought and sold on an exchange like a share.

What is the difference between mutual funds, stocks and ETFs?

The biggest differences are what you own, what price you get and who makes the decisions. The table sets the three side by side.

What is a stock?

A stock, or share, is a small slice of ownership in one company. If the business does well the share can rise, and it may pay dividends, but everything depends on that single company. If it stumbles, your money stumbles with it. The concentration guide shows how large that risk can be.

What is a mutual fund?

A mutual fund is a collective investment: AMFI describes it as money from many investors pooled and invested across things such as shares and bonds. Instead of buying each holding yourself you buy units of the fund, and a fund manager or an index decides what it holds. Your units rise or fall with the value of everything inside it.

NAV, or net asset value, is the price of one unit. AMFI explains that it is worked out at the end of each trading day, by subtracting total liabilities from total assets and dividing by the units outstanding. So if you place an order during the day, you don't know your exact price until later, subject to the fund's cut-off times. Funds also carry a Riskometer, a pictorial meter SEBI requires, with six levels from Low Risk to Very High Risk.

What is an ETF?

An ETF, or exchange traded fund, is a basket of securities that trades on an exchange, usually built to follow an index such as the Nifty 50. NSE describes ETFs as baskets traded like individual stocks, and says that unlike regular open-ended funds they can be bought and sold throughout the trading day. It also notes you can't buy an ETF at the closing NAV the way you can buy a fund. So an ETF gives you a whole basket in one purchase, but you need a broker to buy it, and the price you pay is whatever the market is offering at that moment.

The first ETF on NSE was listed on 8 January 2002. In practice you get a fund's spread across many companies, and you buy and sell it the way you would a share. The cost of that is that you have to think about the things shares involve, such as how many people are trading it and the gap between buyers and sellers, which is why the market order and limit order choice matters for ETFs too. A fund bought at NAV has none of those questions, but you give up knowing your price in advance and trading during the day.

What do these investments cost?

Trading shares and ETFs usually involves brokerage and other trading charges, and funds and ETFs have running costs taken from the fund. Mutual funds also come in two versions.

AMFI explains that Regular and Direct plans of a scheme hold the same portfolio with the same manager, but Regular plans involve distributor commission, so Direct plans generally have a lower expense ratio. SEBI has required a direct plan since 1 January 2013.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a demat account for mutual funds?

For stocks and ETFs you buy through a broker, you need a demat account to hold them. Mutual fund units are issued by the fund, and can also be held outside a demat account.

Is an ETF the same as an index fund?

They can follow the same index, but an ETF trades on the exchange through the day at a market price, while an index fund issues units at the day's NAV.

What is NAV in a mutual fund?

NAV, or net asset value, is the price of one unit. AMFI says it is calculated at the end of each trading day from the fund's assets, liabilities and units outstanding.

Mutual fund, stock aur ETF mein kya fark hai?

Stock ek company ka hissa hai. Mutual fund bahut logon ka paisa ikattha karke invest karta hai aur din ke NAV par units deta hai. ETF ek basket hai jo share ki tarah exchange par din bhar trade hota hai.
