What is concentration risk?

Concentration risk is having too much of your money riding on one outcome. The arithmetic is simple. If half your portfolio is in one stock and it falls 40%, your whole portfolio falls 20%, however well everything else does.

If that stock is 5% of your portfolio, the same fall costs you 2%. The company's news hasn't changed in the second case; what has changed is how much of your future depends on it.

Beginners tend to concentrate without meaning to, because it starts with a stock they like. StockSaathi's own data shows the pull: Reliance Industries alone accounts for 25.1% of first buys. Concentration can also creep up over time, when a holding that rises grows into a large share of your portfolio without you buying any more. It isn't only about single names either: four different banks can behave like one large bet when something hits the whole banking sector. Many stocks don't help if they all depend on one thing.

What does diversification mean?

Diversification means spreading your money so that no single event decides your result. SEBI's financial education booklet describes it as reducing risk by investing across various instruments, industries and other categories, and adds that it doesn't guarantee against loss.

Mutual fund rules show how seriously regulators take this. Under SEBI's Mutual Funds Regulations, no scheme can invest more than 10% of its NAV in the equity shares of any one entity. That limit doesn't apply to index funds, ETFs or sector schemes, which follow their own design. It is a rule for professional managers, and a useful thing to compare with your own portfolio: if a fund manager can't put more than a tenth of a scheme in one company, ask why a beginner would put in half.

What are the common kinds of concentration?

There are three to look for. Single-stock concentration is one company dominating your money. Sector concentration is several companies in the same business, such as IT or banking. Theme concentration is different-looking holdings that share one driver, such as a rising oil price or interest rates.

How many stocks should a beginner hold?

There is no correct number, and StockSaathi doesn't give one. What matters is how differently your holdings behave, not how many there are. A fund or ETF gives you many companies in one purchase, which is one reason beginners look at them. In a practice portfolio, try asking what single piece of bad news would hurt you most, and how much.

Can diversification protect you in a crash?

Only partly. When the whole market falls, most stocks fall together, as the COVID crash guide shows, so a spread of shares doesn't stop a drop. What it protects you from is a single company going badly wrong while the market is fine. Those are the falls that some stocks take years to recover from, if they recover at all.

Frequently asked questions

What is an example of concentration risk?

Holding half your portfolio in one stock. If that stock falls 40%, your whole portfolio falls 20%, whatever your other holdings do.

Does owning many stocks mean I am diversified?

Not always. Several stocks in one sector can move together, so what counts is how differently they behave, not how many you hold.

Does diversification guarantee I won't lose money?

No. SEBI's booklet says it doesn't guarantee against loss. It lowers the damage one holding can do, but a market-wide fall still hits most investments.
