What is a share? A plain-English guide with Indian examples

A share is a small piece of ownership in a company. SEBI's investor booklet says a shareholder is entitled to corporate benefits such as dividends, and to vote at the company's general meeting. If the company does well your share can rise in value, and if it does badly you can lose some or all of your money.

Key takeaways

  • SEBI describes equity shares as a share of ownership in a company.
  • A shareholder can receive dividends out of the company's profits and can vote at general meetings.
  • Reliance Industries closed at ₹1,313.40 on 5 November 2026, and one share is a tiny fraction of a company of that size.
  • A share price moves with what buyers and sellers think the company will be worth, not with what you paid.

What is a share in simple words?

Imagine a business split into a very large number of equal pieces. Each piece is a share, and whoever holds one is a shareholder, a part-owner of the business. SEBI's investor booklet says equity shares, commonly called shares, represent a share of ownership in a company. People use stock and share to mean the same thing in everyday talk.

Companies sell shares to raise money. In the primary market a company issues new shares to investors, through an IPO for example, and the money goes to the company. In the secondary market, investors buy and sell those shares among themselves on a stock exchange, so when you buy on NSE or BSE your money goes to another investor, not to the company.

What does a shareholder get?

SEBI lists two main things. A shareholder is entitled to corporate benefits, such as dividends, which are a part of the company's profit paid out to shareholders. A shareholder also has the right to vote on decisions at the company's general meeting. In practice one share gives you a very small vote, as the table shows, but the right is real, and the rules are the same for a small holder as for a large one.

Companies don't have to pay dividends, though. Many keep their profit to grow the business, so a shareholder's main hope is often that the share price will rise over time.

Why do share prices move?

Because buyers and sellers keep changing their minds about what the company will be worth. When more people want to buy at the current price than to sell, the price rises until the two balance, and the reverse is true for a fall. Results, news, interest rates, the mood of the whole market and plain fear can all shift that balance, and a price can move a lot even when nothing inside the company has changed from one day to the next. This is why the price and the business aren't the same thing.

A share's price tells you what the last buyer and seller agreed on, at that moment, and it says little about what the company will earn over the next ten years. It doesn't tell you whether the share is cheap or dear either, since a ₹100 share isn't cheaper than a ₹5,000 share in any useful sense. For that you need to look at what the company earns, and the P/E ratio guide is a good place to start.

What are the risks of owning shares?

You can lose money, and in a bad case nearly all of it. SEBI's booklet lists market risk, which comes from the overall economy, and risk attached to a particular company or industry. Unlike a bank deposit, a share pays no fixed return, and nothing guarantees you can sell at a price you like.

A share can fall a long way and stay there: the YES Bank guide shows one example. Holding only one or two companies makes that risk larger, as the concentration guide explains.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a stock and a share?

In everyday use they mean the same thing: a piece of ownership in a company. People often say stocks for shares in general, and a share for one unit.

Can you lose all your money in shares?

Yes. A share has no guaranteed value, and a company can fail. Holding one company makes that risk bigger than holding many.

What is a dividend?

A part of a company's profit paid out to its shareholders. Companies don't have to pay one, and many keep profits to grow the business.
