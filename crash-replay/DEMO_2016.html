Demonetisation Shock replay: hold or panic-sell?

Overnight cash ban. Markets panicked briefly, recovered entirely within 4 months, then rallied 30% over the next year. In this replay a ₹1,00,000 portfolio splits in two on day three: one investor holds, the other panic-sells. Holding finished 13.1% ahead of panic-selling.

What happened, day by day

  1. Day 0. Nov 8, 2016, 8 PM. PM Modi announces ₹500 and ₹1000 notes invalid from midnight. Nifty opens down 1.3% next day. Headlines predict chaos.
  2. Day 3. Day 3. Nifty down 5.1%. Cash-heavy businesses are in crisis. You sell at ₹90,700, convinced this is the start of something worse.
  3. Day 16. Nov 24 — Nifty at 7908. Peak panic. News is saturated with queues outside ATMs. In hindsight, this is almost exactly the bottom.
  4. Day 50. By mid-January, the market has fully priced in the news. Digital payments start booming. Holders are back to break-even.
  5. Day 70. 4 months later. Held portfolio: ₹1,02,650 — you're up 2.6%. Panic-sold: still ₹90,700. Over the next 12 months, the Nifty rallied another 27%. Demonetisation felt world-ending, and was forgotten in a quarter.

The lesson

Crashes feel endless from the inside, and the urge to sell is strongest near the bottom. Replaying one lets you feel that pressure with virtual money and see how the decision played out.
