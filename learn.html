Stock market guides for students and beginners in India

Plain-English guides to how the Indian stock market works, written for complete beginners and built on StockSaathi's own data: real Nifty 50 closes, every recorded fall and recovery of Nifty 500 stocks, and the crash replays. Getting started

  • How to learn the stock market as a teenager in India

    The starting point: key words, whether under-18s can invest, and how to practise with virtual money.

  • Can a student open a demat account?

    A student aged 18 or over can open a demat account like any adult, with KYC documents; under 18 a guardian operates the account and the minor can't contract with a broker.

  • Can a teenager open a demat account in India?

    A minor can hold a demat account run by a guardian, but SEBI says a minor can't contract with a broker to buy or sell; trading accounts are sell-only.

  • Got your first salary? How investing works in India (no tips)

    A first salary raises the question of what comes before investing; ₹2,000 a month in the Nifty 50 from January 2015 turned ₹2,84,000 into ₹5,06,648 before costs and dividends.

  • How much money do you need to start investing in India?

    There is no fixed minimum: the floor is one share's price or a fund's minimum; on 5 November 2026 the median NSE share closed at ₹196.90 and SIPs could start from ₹250 or ₹500.

  • How to buy your first share in India, step by step

    To buy your first share in India you need three accounts (bank, trading and demat), complete KYC with a SEBI-registered broker, place an order, and receive the shares after settlement.

  • How to invest as a student in India

    Students under 18 can't trade through a broker but can learn with virtual money; adults can open accounts with KYC. There is no fixed minimum, and learning first costs nothing.

  • Share market kaise sikhe: bina paise ka 4 hafte ka practice plan

    A Hinglish four-week plan to learn the share market with virtual money: key words, first practice trades, a crash replay and reviewing your own mistakes.

  • What is a demat account?

    A demat account holds shares in electronic form through a Depository Participant of NSDL or CDSL; a Basic Services Demat Account covers holdings up to ₹10 lakh.

  • What is a share? A plain-English guide with Indian examples

    A share is a small piece of ownership in a company, which SEBI says entitles you to corporate benefits like dividends and a vote; one Reliance share is a tiny fraction of the company.

How the market works

  • Large cap, mid cap and small cap explained

    In India large cap means the top 100 companies by full market value, mid cap the next 150 and small cap the rest; in June 2026 the cut-offs were about ₹1,06,346 crore and ₹33,664 crore.

  • Market order, limit order and AMO explained, with examples

    A market order fills now at the best available price, a limit order only at your price or better, and an AMO is placed while the market is closed and runs at the next open.

  • Nifty 50 and Sensex: what they are and how they're calculated

    The Nifty 50 tracks 50 large NSE companies and the Sensex 30 on the BSE, both weighted by free-float market value; the Nifty rose 265.8% from November 2010 to October 2026.

  • NSE and BSE explained: what is the difference?

    NSE and BSE are India's two main stock exchanges; many companies list on both, so one share can show two slightly different prices at the same moment.

  • What is the P/E ratio? Explained with real Indian stocks

    The P/E ratio is a share's price divided by its earnings per share; on 6 November 2026 eight large Indian companies ranged from 10.47 to 49.99.

  • T+1 settlement in India explained, with worked examples

    Indian stock trades settle one working day after the trade (T+1), for all stocks since 27 January 2023; optional same-day T+0 began on 28 March 2024.

Market crashes and recoveries

  • The 2008 crash in India: why selling early won, and why that's rare

    In 2008 the Nifty 50 fell 59.9% in ten months, so selling early won; it took until November 2010 to get back, and no fall since has been as deep.

  • Adani and Hindenburg 2023: a share price timeline

    Five Adani group shares fell between 39% and 77% from 24 January 2023 to their 2023 lows; by October 2026 two stood well above that level and three below.

  • What happened to ₹1 lakh in the Nifty 50 during the COVID crash (2020)

    ₹1,00,000 in the Nifty 50 fell 37.2% in 33 days in 2020; holding ended at ₹1,02,764 by 9 Nov 2020, selling on day three at ₹97,295.

  • Demonetisation and the stock market: what happened from November 2016 to February 2017

    After demonetisation on 8 November 2016 the Nifty 50 fell 7.4% to its low on 26 December 2016 and was back above its starting level by 25 January 2017.

  • Large-cap stocks that recovered fastest from falls since 2011

    Across 86 Nifty 100 companies with at least five recovered 10% falls since 2011, the typical median recovery was 67.5 trading days, from 25 for the fastest to 318 for the slowest.

  • The Harshad Mehta scam explained for students

    The 1992 securities scam involved banks' government securities deals and money diverted to brokers' accounts; a Special Court ordinance followed on 6 June 1992.

  • The Nifty 50's biggest one-day falls

    The Nifty 50's biggest one-day fall since 2007 was 13.0% on 23 March 2020; six of the ten worst days came in 2008 and three in 2020.

  • How long does the Nifty 50 take to recover from a 10%, 20% or 30% fall?

    Every 10%+ fall in the Nifty 50 since its November 2010 high with its recovery time: the median was 200 trading days; the only 30% fall was COVID-19 in 2020.

  • The market just fell 10%. What does history say?

    After its first 10% fall, the Nifty 50 fell a median of 6.5 points further in nine falls since 2010: three stopped before 15% and three passed 20%.

  • India's biggest stock market crashes, dated and measured

    Since 2007 the Nifty 50 has had four falls of 20% or more: 59.9% in 2008, 38.4% in 2020, 27.9% in 2010 to 2011 and 22.5% in 2015 to 2016.

  • Nifty 500 stocks still below their last peak

    Of 423 Indian stocks with clean data, 89.6% were 10% or more below their highest close since 2011 at the end of October 2026 and 23.2% were at least half below it.

  • What is a market correction?

    A market correction is a fall of about 10% to 20%; the Nifty 50 has had six since 2007, with a median depth of 15.1% and about 153 sessions from peak to recovery.

  • YES Bank 2020: what a bank collapse looks like in the share price

    YES Bank shares fell 96% from their August 2018 high to the moratorium in March 2020, and most of that fall came before the headline day.

Investing behaviour

  • 52-week highs, lows and the anchoring trap

    A 52-week high is a stock's highest price of the last year; on 30 October 2026 half the Nifty 50 was 20% or more below it, and anchoring on that number is a common trap.

  • Diversification for beginners: why putting most of your money in one stock is risky

    Of 423 large and mid-sized Indian stocks, 53.2% were 30% or more below their high in late 2026, against 12.4% for the Nifty 50, which is why one stock is riskier than a basket.

  • The disposition effect: selling winners too early

    The disposition effect is the tendency to sell winning investments too soon and hold losers too long, documented by Terrance Odean in 10,000 brokerage accounts.

  • FOMO in the stock market: what it is, and what happened after big run-ups

    After 463 sharp run-ups in large Indian stocks since 2011, the typical next 60 sessions were not worse than normal, but outcomes spread far wider in both directions.

  • The sectors Indian students trade most

    On StockSaathi, energy, banking and IT services make up 42.6% of all practice trades, and 56.8% of trades are in Nifty 50 companies.

  • Overtrading and churning: when more trades means worse results

    ₹1,00,000 in the Nifty 50 from 2011 reached ₹3,75,794 by October 2026 but only ₹2,29,254 without its 10 best days; frequent trading makes missing such days likelier.

  • Panic selling: what it is, why beginners do it, and how to stop

    Panic selling is selling out of fear during a fast fall; StockSaathi's coach flags it after the trade, and 99.7% of sales on StockSaathi since 25 Oct 2026 didn't fit the pattern.

  • What Indian students buy first when they start investing

    On StockSaathi, Reliance Industries is the most common first buy (25.1% of first trades) and 67.9% of first buys are Nifty 50 companies.

Funds and long-term investing

  • ELSS and tax-saving mutual funds explained

    ELSS is an equity mutual fund with a statutory 3-year lock-in; the deduction up to ₹1,50,000 applies only if you opt out of the default tax regime under the Income-tax Act, 2025.

  • Index funds and active funds explained

    An index fund copies an index and an active fund tries to beat one; S&P's mid-2026 report found 85% of Indian large-cap funds lagged their benchmark over 5 years.

  • Mutual fund costs explained: expense ratio and exit load

    An expense ratio is a fund's yearly running cost and an exit load a fee for leaving early; under SEBI's 2026 rules an exit load can't exceed 3% of NAV.

  • Mutual funds for beginners in India: how they work

    A mutual fund pools investors' money and issues units at NAV; AMFI's file for 5 November 2026 shows 8,709 plans from 55 fund houses with a recent NAV, regulated by SEBI.

  • Mutual funds, stocks and ETFs explained for beginners

    A stock is a share of one company, a mutual fund pools money and issues units at the day's NAV, and an ETF is a basket that trades on an exchange like a share.

  • What is a SIP? Explained with a real Nifty 50 backtest

    ₹5,000 a month in the Nifty 50 from January 2015 to October 2026 put in ₹7,10,000 and reached ₹12,66,619, a yearly return of 9.4% before costs and dividends.
