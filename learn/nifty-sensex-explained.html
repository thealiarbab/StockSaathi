Nifty 50 and Sensex: what they are and how they're calculated

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 2 November 2026

The Nifty 50 and the Sensex are India's two best-known stock market indices. The Nifty 50 tracks 50 large, frequently traded companies on the NSE, and the Sensex tracks 30 on the BSE. Both are weighted by each company's free-float market value, so when people say "the market rose today", they usually mean one of these.

Key takeaways

  • The Nifty 50 tracks 50 large NSE companies chosen from the Nifty 100; the Sensex tracks 30 BSE companies chosen from the BSE 100.
  • Both are weighted by free-float market value, so the biggest companies move them the most.
  • The Nifty 50 started at 1,000 on 3 November 1995; the Sensex measures against a base of 100 in the 1978 to 1979 financial year.
  • From its record close on 9 November 2010 to 30 October 2026 the Nifty 50 rose from 6,301.55 to 23,053.10, up 265.8%.
  • It ended 13 of the 15 full calendar years from 2011 to 2025 higher; the exceptions were 2011, down 24.6%, and 2015, down 4.1%.

What is the difference between the Nifty 50 and the Sensex?

They measure the same thing, the big end of the Indian stock market, from two different exchanges. The table compares them using each index provider's own methodology document.

How is the Nifty 50 calculated?

The Nifty 50 adds up the free-float market capitalisation of its 50 companies and compares the total with its starting point. Market capitalisation is a company's share price multiplied by its number of shares. Free float counts only the shares that ordinary investors can actually buy and sell, leaving out large holdings such as a promoter's stake, so a company where most shares are locked up counts for less.

The index started at 1,000 on 3 November 1995. A reading of 23,053.10, its close on 30 October 2026, means the free-float value of its members, adjusted for changes to the list over the years, is about 23 times what it was at the start. The providers adjust the calculation for events like share splits and changes of members so that the index moves only when prices move.

How are companies chosen for the Nifty 50 and Sensex?

By size and how easily they trade. NSE's methodology picks the Nifty 50 from the Nifty 100 by free-float market value, keeping only companies whose shares can be traded cheaply in large amounts and that have derivative contracts on NSE. The Sensex is drawn from the BSE 100 and fixed at 30 companies, each listed on BSE for at least six months and traded on every day of the review period. Both lists are reviewed regularly, so companies that shrink can drop out and growing ones can join.

How has the Nifty 50 done each year since 2011?

From November 2010 the index rose 265.8%, and 13 of the 15 full calendar years ended higher. But the yearly numbers hide how bumpy the path was: the index fell 10% or more nine times in that period, including 38.4% in the COVID-19 crash of 2020, which still ended as an up year. The record of every Nifty fall since 2010 shows how long each took to recover. Past years say nothing certain about the next one.

Why do the Nifty and Sensex usually move together?

Because both are built from India's largest, most traded companies and both give the most weight to the biggest of them, so on most days they rise or fall by similar percentages. Their point values are very different only because they started at different times and bases: a Sensex point and a Nifty point are not comparable, but their daily percentage moves are. What do index points mean?

An index point is a unit of the index's value, not rupees. The Nifty 50 began at 1,000 and the Sensex at 100, so a reading today tells you how far the market value of the group has moved since those starting points. That is why percentages are the useful number: 200 points meant far more when the Nifty stood at 6,000 than it does near 23,000.

What does free-float mean?

Free-float means the shares that are actually available for the public to trade, leaving out large blocks that promoters and similar long-term holders keep. Both indices weight companies by free-float market value, so a company counts for the value of its tradable shares rather than all its shares.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Nifty 50?

The Nifty 50 is the NSE's main stock market index. It tracks 50 large companies chosen from the Nifty 100 by free-float market value and trading liquidity, and it started at 1,000 on 3 November 1995.

What is the Sensex?

The Sensex is BSE's main index. It tracks 30 large companies chosen from the BSE 100, weighted by free-float market value, measured against a base of 100 in 1978-79. It was launched on 2 January 1986.
