India's biggest stock market crashes, dated and measured

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

Since 2007 the Nifty 50 has fallen 20% or more four times. The deepest was 59.9% in 2008, followed by 38.4% in 2020, 27.9% in 2010 to 2011 and 22.5% in 2015 to 2016. Every one recovered, but it took from less than a year to almost three, and nothing guarantees the next will.

Key takeaways

  • The Nifty 50 has had 4 falls of 20% or more since 2007.
  • The deepest was 59.9% in 2008, and it took 694 sessions from peak to recovery.
  • The fastest of the four was COVID-19: 38.4% down, and back at the high 205 sessions after the peak.
  • The two quieter falls, 27.9% and 22.5%, were slow grinds that took longer to recover than COVID-19 despite being far shallower.

What happened in the 2008 crash?

The deepest fall of the period came from the global financial crisis. The Nifty lost 59.9% between January and October 2008, and only regained its high on 9 November 2010. It is also the crash that breaks the usual story: selling early came out well ahead. The 2008 guide covers it in detail.

What happened in the COVID-19 crash?

This was the fastest. The World Health Organization described COVID-19 as a pandemic on 11 March 2020, and the Nifty hit its low twelve days later, 38.4% below its January high. It was also the quickest of the four to recover, closing back at the high by 9 November 2020. The COVID crash guide follows ₹1,00,000 through it.

What were the two slower falls?

They rarely make headlines, but they tested people in a different way. From November 2010 the Nifty slid 27.9% over 271 sessions, and it wasn't back at its high until November 2013, nearly three years after the peak. In 2015 and 2016 it fell 22.5% over 241 sessions. Neither had one famous day to point to.

What patterns repeat?

Three things show up across all four. First, how far a market falls doesn't tell you how long it will take to recover: the 2010 to 2011 slide was less than half as deep as 2008, yet took even longer to get back. Speed on the way down didn't predict much either, since COVID-19 fell fastest and recovered fastest. Second, the worst single days arrive in clusters, as the list of the worst sessions shows, and six of the ten worst days fell in 2008.

Third, every one of these falls ended in recovery for the index, but that is a statement about a basket of the largest companies, whose members change. Shares of individual companies had a much rougher time, and many remain below old highs. Four falls in nineteen years is also a very small sample. It gives you a feel for the range of what has happened, not a forecast of what will.

What about crashes before 2007?

Our daily data starts in September 2007, so we can't measure earlier falls the same way. The best-known earlier episode is the securities scam of 1992, which changed how Indian markets are regulated. Rather than repeat index figures we couldn't confirm from an official source, that guide sticks to what the RBI and the Supreme Court recorded.

How do smaller falls fit in?

Six further falls in this period reached 10% but stopped before 20%. Those are market corrections. Demonetisation in 2016 never reached 10% at all, though it felt serious at the time.

Frequently asked questions

What was the biggest stock market crash in India?

Since September 2007, when this data begins, it was the 2008 crisis: the Nifty 50 fell 59.9% from 8 January to 27 October 2008.

How long did Indian crashes take to recover?

Measured from peak back to peak, the four falls of 20% or more took between 205 sessions (COVID-19) and 728 sessions (2010 to 2011).

Will the market always recover from a crash?

The Nifty 50 recovered from all four, but past recoveries don't guarantee future ones, and individual shares often do much worse than the index.

India mein sabse bada share market crash kab hua?
