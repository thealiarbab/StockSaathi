Large-cap stocks after a fall: how fast do they recover?

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

The typical large Indian company climbed back from a 10% fall in a median of 67.5 trading days, about three months, in data since 2011. The range is huge: from 25 trading days for the fastest stock to 318 for the slowest. A company's size doesn't tell you how quickly its shares will return.

Key takeaways

  • Across 86 Nifty 100 companies, the typical stock's median recovery from a 10% fall was 67.5 trading days.
  • The fastest stock's median was 25 trading days and the slowest was 318.
  • The Nifty 50 index itself had a median of 200 trading days, because its 10% falls are rarer and run deeper.
  • Only stocks with at least 5 recovered falls are included.
  • Falls that never recovered aren't counted in these medians, so the true picture is less comfortable.

Which large caps recovered fastest and slowest?

The table ranks companies by the median number of trading days their shares took to get back to the old high, counted from the day a fall first reached 10%. It describes what happened; it isn't a list of good or bad investments.

What does recovery mean for a stock?

Here it means the share closed back at or above the high it had before the fall. A 10% fall counts once a close is at least 10% below the most recent high, and the clock starts that day. It stops the day the stock regains that high. Falls still open don't have a recovery time yet, so they are left out of the median.

A large cap here is a company in the Nifty 100, which in practice means the biggest listed companies by market value. This is today's list, so it doesn't include companies that were large in 2011 and have since dropped out.

Why does a single stock recover faster than the Nifty?

It usually doesn't mean stocks are safer than the index. It reflects how often each one falls. A single share is much more volatile than a basket of 50, so it drops 10% often, including on ordinary bumps that reverse in weeks.

The Nifty 50 only falls 10% when something broad is happening, which is why it has had just nine such falls since its November 2010 high, against seventeen or more in some single stocks here. So the index's median of 200 trading days covers a small set of serious falls, while a stock's median includes many small ones.

The two numbers measure different things, and comparing them directly would mislead you. The Nifty recovery guide has the index's full record. A better way to read this table is to look at the stocks one at a time: how often they fell and how long they took each time. A company with many quick recoveries has behaved very differently from one with a handful of very long ones, even if both are household names.

Why do some large caps take so much longer?

There is no single reason, and this data doesn't prove one. Several of the slowest names are in businesses tied to commodity prices, government policy or long cycles, where a downturn can last for years. Faster names in this period include companies whose earnings were growing quickly. Those are patterns to look into, not causes this table can establish, and a stock's past speed says nothing certain about its next fall.

What are the limits of this data?

The biggest limit is that only recovered falls count. Many large companies are still below an old high, and the stock pages on StockSaathi show which falls are still open. The list is also today's Nifty 100, so it leaves out companies that fell far enough to leave the list. Both effects make recovery look more reliable than it really is, which is another reason to keep any one holding from becoming too large.

Frequently asked questions

How long do large-cap stocks take to recover from a fall?

Across 86 Nifty 100 companies since 2011, the typical median recovery from a 10% fall was 67.5 trading days, about three months, but it ranged from 25 to 318 for individual stocks.

Do all large-cap stocks recover?

No. These medians count only falls that recovered. Some falls are still open, and a large company can stay below an old high for years.

Should I buy a stock because it recovers quickly?

StockSaathi doesn't recommend stocks. A fast past recovery describes what happened, and it can't tell you what the next fall will do.
