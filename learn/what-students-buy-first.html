What Indian students buy first when they start investing

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 2 November 2026

On StockSaathi, a free practice app built for students, the most common first buy is Reliance Industries, chosen in 25.1% of first trades. Big, familiar names dominate: 67.9% of first buys are Nifty 50 companies, led by Reliance, TCS, HDFC Bank and Bharti Airtel. Familiar is not the same as well understood, though.

Key takeaways

  • Reliance Industries is the most common first buy on StockSaathi, chosen in 25.1% of first trades.
  • The next four are TCS (6.3%), HDFC Bank (6.2%), Bharti Airtel (5.9%) and State Bank of India (3.8%).
  • 67.9% of first buys are Nifty 50 companies, and only 2.4% are ETFs.
  • Across all trades, 56.8% are in Nifty 50 companies.
  • Energy (17.3%), banking (14.9%) and IT services (10.4%) are the most-traded sectors.

What do beginners buy first on StockSaathi?

The table ranks the companies people chose for their very first purchase. Percentages are shares of all first buys; StockSaathi publishes only percentages and rankings, never counts or anything about an individual.

Why do beginners pick the same few companies?

Because they are the names people already know. Reliance, TCS, HDFC Bank and Airtel are household brands, they are in the news every week, and they are among the largest companies in the Nifty 50, the index of 50 large NSE companies. When you have no other way to choose, picking a company you recognise feels safe. On StockSaathi the pull is strong: one company, Reliance, accounts for a quarter of all first buys.

Recognition is a reasonable place to start learning, because it is easier to follow news about a company whose products you use. But knowing a brand is not the same as understanding the business behind it: how it earns money, how much debt it carries, or what is already priced into its shares. SEBI's investor booklet makes the same point when it tells investors not to act on market sentiment but to look at the company and its financials.

Which sectors do students trade the most?

Energy leads with 17.3% of all trades, largely because Reliance is classed as an energy company, followed by banking at 14.9% and IT services at 10.4%. Telecom and finance companies come next. A sector is a group of companies in the same line of business, such as banks or software firms; sectors matter because companies in one sector often rise and fall together.

The pattern mirrors the first buys: most activity sits in a handful of very large companies. Across all trades, 56.8% are in Nifty 50 companies, and only 2.4% of first buys are ETFs, the funds that hold a whole index in one purchase.

What does this mean for someone starting out?

It shows a common starting habit, not a recommendation. StockSaathi never tells anyone what to buy, and the most popular first stock is not a better investment because it is popular. What the data does show is how quickly a beginner's money can end up concentrated. If a large part of your practice portfolio sits in one company, or in one sector, a single bad week for that name moves everything you own.

That is one of the nine patterns StockSaathi's coach checks for: it flags a trade that pushes one stock past 40% of your portfolio, or one sector past 55%. A useful first experiment is to look at what you actually own after a few trades and ask whether it is a portfolio or a bet on one brand.
