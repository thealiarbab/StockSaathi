NSE and BSE explained: what's the difference?

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

NSE and BSE are India's two main stock exchanges, the marketplaces where shares are bought and sold. BSE dates from 1875, while NSE was incorporated in 1992 and began trading shares in 1994. Many companies list on both, so for most beginners the choice of exchange makes little practical difference.

Key takeaways

  • BSE, established in 1875, describes itself as Asia's first stock exchange.
  • NSE was incorporated in 1992 and started trading equities on 3 November 1994.
  • StockSaathi's list counts 2,647 NSE main-board stocks, 583 NSE small-company (SME) stocks and 1,318 stocks found only on BSE.
  • 2,520 of those NSE main-board stocks also carry a BSE code, so they trade on both exchanges.
  • The Nifty 50 is NSE's main index and the Sensex is BSE's.

What is the difference between NSE and BSE?

They are separate exchanges doing the same job. Each one runs its own market where buyers' and sellers' orders meet, and each has its own main index.

What are NSE and BSE?

A stock exchange is a regulated marketplace where shares are traded. BSE Ltd., formerly the Bombay Stock Exchange, was established in 1875. NSE, the National Stock Exchange, was incorporated in 1992, recognised as a stock exchange by SEBI in April 1993, and began with its wholesale debt market before equities trading started on 3 November 1994.

Both are overseen by SEBI, the Securities and Exchange Board of India, which regulates India's securities markets. You don't deal with an exchange directly: you place your order through a registered broker, and the broker sends it to the exchange.

Why can one stock show two prices?

Because each exchange has its own market. When a company lists on both NSE and BSE, its shares trade separately on each, with different buyers and sellers placing orders at any moment. The last traded price on one can therefore differ slightly from the other, and you may see a share at ₹1,000.20 on NSE and ₹1,000.05 on BSE at the same moment. It is the same company and the same kind of share, and only the marketplace differs.

These gaps are usually tiny for large, heavily traded companies, because many people are watching both prices and trading on each side, which pulls them back together. They can be wider in thinly traded shares, where a single order can move the price. For a beginner, the useful habit is to check which exchange a quoted price comes from before comparing two numbers, because a small difference between two apps may just mean they are showing different exchanges, or different moments in time, rather than an error in either one.

Does it matter which exchange you use?

For a beginner buying a large company, usually not much. Most brokers let you trade on either, and shares bought on one exchange end up in the same demat account as shares bought on the other. It matters more when a stock is only on one exchange, which is true of the 1,318 stocks on StockSaathi's list found only on BSE, or when one exchange has far more trading in a share than the other, since more trading usually means orders fill more easily.

The order you choose matters more than the exchange. The guide to market and limit orders explains why.

What are the Nifty and Sensex?

They are the two best-known scorecards for the market, not exchanges themselves. The Nifty 50 tracks 50 large companies on NSE, and the Sensex tracks 30 on BSE. The Nifty and Sensex guide explains how each is built and why they move together.

What are the market timings on NSE?

NSE's normal market runs from 9:15 am to 3:30 pm on trading days, after a pre-open session from 9:00 am to 9:08 am. For stocks with derivative contracts, NSE has also introduced a Closing Auction Session that runs as a separate session from 3:15 pm to 3:35 pm, so don't assume every stock simply stops trading at 3:30 pm.

What are NSE Emerge and the BSE SME platform?

They are separate platforms for smaller companies. NSE launched Emerge, its SME platform, on 18 September 2012, and BSE also runs a platform for small and medium enterprises. Shares here tend to trade less often and can be riskier, so the same company size that makes them interesting can make them harder to sell when you want to.

Frequently asked questions

Which is older, NSE or BSE?

BSE. It was established in 1875, while NSE was incorporated in 1992 and began trading equities on 3 November 1994.

Can I buy on NSE and sell on BSE?

Ask your broker. Shares from both exchanges sit in the same demat account, but how a sale on the other exchange is handled depends on the broker and the timing.

Is SEBI the same as NSE or BSE?

No. NSE and BSE are stock exchanges. SEBI is the regulator that recognises and oversees them.
