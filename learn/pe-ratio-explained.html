What is the P/E ratio? Explained with real Indian stocks

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

The P/E ratio is a share's price divided by the company's earnings per share. It tells you how many rupees buyers are paying today for each rupee of yearly profit. A share at ₹500 earning ₹25 a share has a P/E of 20. It describes a price; it doesn't say whether that price is right.

Key takeaways

  • NSE defines the price-earnings ratio as the ratio of a company's share price to its earnings per share.
  • A share at ₹500 with earnings per share of ₹25 has a P/E of 20.
  • On 6 November 2026, eight large Indian companies ranged from a P/E of 10.47 to 49.99.
  • P/E differs widely between sectors, so it is most useful for comparing similar companies.
  • The Nifty's own P/E uses its companies' earnings over the last 4 quarters.

What is the P/E ratio?

P/E stands for price to earnings. Earnings per share, or EPS, is a company's profit divided by the number of its shares, so it is the slice of yearly profit that belongs to one share. Divide the share price by that slice and you get the P/E. A P/E of 20 means buyers are paying ₹20 for every ₹1 the company earned.

SEBI's investor material lists EPS and the P/E ratio among the company financials to look at before you buy. That is the right way to think of it: one thing to check among several, not an answer on its own.

How do you calculate the P/E ratio?

Divide the price by the earnings per share. Take a company with 10 crore shares that made ₹250 crore in profit: its EPS is ₹25. If the share trades at ₹500, its P/E is 500 ÷ 25 = 20.

If the price rises to ₹600 and profit stays the same, the P/E becomes 24. If profit rises to ₹300 crore and the price stays at ₹500, EPS becomes ₹30 and the P/E falls to about 16.7.

So a P/E can fall because the price dropped or because earnings grew, and those two stories mean very different things. Always look at which number moved.

Is a high or low P/E ratio better?

Neither is better on its own. A high P/E usually means buyers expect profits to grow, or see the business as steady and are willing to pay more for that. A low P/E can mean the shares are cheap, but it can also mean buyers expect profits to fall, or see more risk in the business. The number alone can't tell you which of these is true, which is why there is no single "good" P/E that works for every company.

In the table, the highest figure is nearly five times the lowest, and both are very large, well-known companies. Comparing Asian Paints with State Bank of India says little, because a paint maker and a bank earn money in completely different ways. Comparing TCS with Infosys, or HDFC Bank with State Bank of India, tells you more. Even then, a gap between two similar companies is a question to look into, not a verdict on which is the better investment.

What is the difference between trailing and forward P/E?

Trailing P/E uses earnings a company has already reported, usually over the last four quarters. Forward P/E uses earnings that analysts estimate for the future. Trailing figures are facts about the past, while forward figures are guesses that can turn out wrong. When you see a P/E quoted, check which kind it is.

An index has a P/E too. NSE works out the Nifty's P/E as the index's market value divided by the earnings its companies reported over the trailing four quarters, and says it can be used as a comparison benchmark.

What are the limits of the P/E ratio?

It breaks down in several common cases. A company making a loss has no meaningful P/E. A one-off gain, such as selling a business, can inflate earnings for a year and make the P/E look low. Companies whose profits swing with a cycle can look cheapest at the peak of their earnings, just before those earnings fall.

P/E also says nothing about debt, cash or how the company is run. It is also tied to the price, so comparing today's price with an old high or buying because a price has run up are separate traps that this one number won't warn you about.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good P/E ratio?

There is no single good number. P/E depends on the sector and on how fast profits are expected to grow, so it is most useful for comparing a company with similar companies or with its own past.

Can a P/E ratio be negative?

If a company makes a loss, its earnings per share are negative and the ratio stops meaning anything. Most data providers show no P/E in that case.

Is the P/E ratio enough to decide whether to buy a share?

No. It is one measure of price against past profit. SEBI's investor material lists it alongside others, and it says nothing about debt, competition or how the business will do next.

PE ratio kya hota hai?

PE ratio share ki price ko earnings per share se divide karke milta hai. Agar share ₹500 ka hai aur EPS ₹25 hai, to PE 20 hua, yaani log har ₹1 ke profit ke liye ₹20 de rahe hain.
