What is a SIP? Explained with a real Nifty 50 backtest

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

A SIP, or systematic investment plan, puts a fixed amount into a mutual fund at regular intervals. Investing ₹5,000 a month in the Nifty 50 from January 2015 to October 2026 meant putting in ₹7,10,000, which reached ₹12,66,619. That works out to about 9.4% a year, before costs and dividends.

Key takeaways

  • AMFI defines a SIP as investing a fixed amount in a mutual fund scheme periodically at fixed intervals.

What are SIP returns on the Nifty 50?

About 9.4% a year before costs and dividends, on ₹5,000 a month from January 2015 to October 2026. The table is a backtest: it applies a monthly ₹5,000 to the real Nifty 50 close on the first session of each month. Look at the first row as well as the last, because a SIP can show a loss for a long time.

What is a SIP?

AMFI describes a SIP as an investment method offered by mutual funds, where you invest a fixed amount in a scheme at fixed intervals, such as every month. It is a way of buying, not a type of investment: the money still goes into a fund, and it rises and falls with what the fund holds. In practice, a set amount leaves your bank account on a chosen date and buys units at that day's NAV.

Beware of one thing when you search for this term: the same initials are used for student internship and study programmes too. In investing, SIP always means this regular-investment method.

How does rupee cost averaging work?

Because the amount is fixed and the price moves, you automatically buy more units when the price is low and fewer when it is high. This is called rupee cost averaging. Say you put in ₹1,000 a month. At a NAV of ₹10 you get 100 units, at ₹8 you get 125 units, and at ₹12.50 you get 80 units.

After three months you hold 305 units for ₹3,000, an average cost of about ₹9.84 a unit, which is lower than the ₹10.17 average of the three prices. It doesn't stop you from losing money if the price falls and stays down, and it doesn't pick good moments for you.

What it does is take the timing decision away, so you don't have to guess each month whether today is a good day to buy. That matters more for beginners than it sounds, because the temptation to stop buying is strongest exactly when prices are lowest and the news is worst. A SIP that carries on through a fall keeps buying at those low prices, as the COVID crash guide shows. It is a discipline, and it only works if you keep going.

What is the difference between a SIP and a lump sum?

A lump sum goes in all at once and a SIP goes in over time. In this backtest, investing the whole ₹7,10,000 on the first day would have reached ₹19,49,592, well ahead of the SIP's ₹12,66,619, because the index rose over the period and money invested earlier had longer to grow. A market that fell after the start would have reversed that result.

The comparison is also a little unfair. Most people, and almost every student, don't have the total on day one: they have a monthly income or allowance, so a SIP often isn't a choice against a lump sum at all. It is simply how regular money gets invested.

Can a SIP lose money?

Yes. In this backtest the plan was down 6.1% after its first year, and it fell from ₹13,87,352 in January 2026 to ₹12,66,619 by the end of October, even after more money went in. A SIP takes on the full risk of whatever the fund holds. The record of Nifty falls shows how long those stretches have lasted.

Can students start a SIP with a small amount?

The amounts can be small: AMFI says a SIP instalment could be as small as ₹500 a month, and ₹250 a month under Chhoti SIP. What is possible depends on the fund and on your age. Under 18, you can't open one on your own, and any investment has to go through a guardian. Students can learn the habit first with virtual money.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum amount for a SIP?

AMFI says the instalment could be as small as ₹500 a month, and ₹250 a month under Chhoti SIP. The minimum depends on the scheme.

What return did a Nifty 50 SIP give?

₹5,000 a month from January 2015 to October 2026 gave about 9.4% a year on the index alone, before costs and dividends. A different period would give a different number.

Is a SIP better than a lump sum?

Neither always wins. In this rising period a lump sum on day one ended higher, but a SIP suits money that arrives each month and spreads your buying over time.

SIP kya hota hai?

SIP matlab har mahine ya tay samay par mutual fund mein ek fixed amount lagana. Price kam ho to zyada units milte hain aur zyada ho to kam, lekin nuksan bhi ho sakta hai.
