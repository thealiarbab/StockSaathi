YES Bank 2020: what a bank collapse looks like in the share price

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

YES Bank's shares fell from ₹394.00 in August 2018 to ₹16.15 on 6 March 2020, the day after the RBI put the bank under moratorium. Most of that fall happened before the headline: by the end of 2019 the shares had already lost 88.1%. In October 2026 they were still far below the old high.

Key takeaways

  • YES Bank closed at ₹394.00 on 20 August 2018, its highest close in this data.
  • By 31 December 2019, more than two months before the moratorium, the shares were already 88.1% below that high.
  • The shares fell 56.1% in one session on 6 March 2020, to ₹16.15.
  • On 30 October 2026 they closed at ₹15.15, still 96.2% below the 2018 high.

What is a bank moratorium?

A moratorium is a legal freeze on a bank's normal business for a set period, used to stop the position getting worse while a solution is worked out. On 5 March 2020 the Central Government imposed one on YES Bank under section 45 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, on the RBI's application. The RBI said it would draw up a reconstruction scheme well before the 30 days ended.

The same day the RBI superseded the bank's board for 30 days and appointed an administrator. On 6 March it published a draft reconstruction scheme, and said State Bank of India had expressed its willingness to invest.

How did the rescue end?

Quickly. The Government notified the Yes Bank Ltd. Reconstruction Scheme, 2020 on 13 March, and the moratorium was lifted with effect from 18 March, well before its original end date of 3 April. The bank survived and kept operating.

The share price tells a different story from the bank's. A rescue protects a bank and its customers, but it doesn't promise a shareholder their money back. The shares jumped in the days after the scheme, then drifted lower, closing at ₹10.80 in August 2021.

More than six years after the moratorium they were still more than 95% below the 2018 high. Someone who bought in the rebound of March 2020 at ₹58.65 and held to October 2026 would have been sitting on a loss of about 74%. That is why this guide is about the share price and not only about the bank: the two aren't the same thing.

Why did the fall come before the news?

Prices move as people change their minds, and that usually happens long before an official announcement. By the end of 2019 the market had already marked the shares down by 88.1%. The moratorium was the event everyone remembers, but a shareholder watching only for a big headline would have missed nearly all of the decline.

This doesn't mean a fall always signals trouble. Most falls in large companies do recover. It is that a very long, steady slide in one company is worth a closer look, and that "it has already fallen so much" is not a reason on its own. The anchoring guide explains why an old high can mislead you.

What does it teach about owning one stock?

Size your risk so that one company can't sink you. A portfolio with a small holding in YES Bank was hurt. A portfolio built around it was close to wiped out, and that outcome was decided long before March 2020, on the day the holding grew too large. The concentration guide shows the arithmetic.

Frequently asked questions

Why did YES Bank shares crash in 2020?

The shares had been sliding since 2018, and were already 88.1% below their high by the end of 2019. They fell a further 56.1% in one session on 6 March 2020, after the bank was placed under moratorium on 5 March.

Did YES Bank shares recover?

Not to the old high. They rebounded in March 2020, then fell again, and on 30 October 2026 closed at ₹15.15, 96.2% below the 2018 high.

What is a moratorium on a bank?

A legal freeze on a bank's normal business for a set period. YES Bank's was imposed on 5 March 2020 and lifted with effect from 18 March 2020.

YES Bank ka share itna kyun gira?

Share 2018 se lagatar gir raha tha aur 2019 ke ant tak 88.1% neeche aa chuka tha. 5 March 2020 ko moratorium laga, aur agle din share ek hi session mein 56.1% gir gaya.
