ELSS and tax-saving mutual funds explained

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

An ELSS, or Equity Linked Saving Scheme, is an equity mutual fund with a statutory 3-year lock-in and a tax benefit. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, the related deduction of up to ₹1,50,000 isn't available under the default tax regime. So whether it helps you depends on which regime you use.

Key takeaways

  • SEBI defines an ELSS as an open-ended equity linked saving scheme with a statutory lock-in of 3 years and tax benefit, holding at least 80% in equity.
  • Section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 gives a deduction of up to ₹1,50,000 for items listed in Schedule XV.
  • That deduction isn't available under the default regime; you have to opt out of it.
  • Long-term gains on equity funds above ₹1,25,000 are taxed at 12.5%, where the conditions in section 198 are met.

How does ELSS compare with an ordinary equity fund?

The main differences are the lock-in and the tax treatment. The market risk is the same.

What is an ELSS fund?

ELSS stands for Equity Linked Saving Scheme. SEBI's scheme categories describe it as an open-ended equity linked saving scheme with a statutory lock-in of 3 years and tax benefit, with at least 80% of total assets in equity and equity-related instruments, in line with the Equity Linked Saving Scheme, 2005 notified by the Ministry of Finance. It is a mutual fund first and a tax product second: your money rises and falls with the shares the fund holds.

How does the tax deduction work now?

The law changed its numbering, so older guides can mislead you. Older material refers to Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The Income Tax Department's current pages show the Income-tax Act, 2025, where section 123 allows an individual or HUF a deduction of up to ₹1,50,000 for sums listed in Schedule XV. That schedule covers items such as life insurance premiums and provident fund contributions, and it includes units of a mutual fund under a plan notified by the Central Government.

The key point for a beginner is the regime. Under section 202 of the 2025 Act, tax is worked out by default at the slab rates in that section, and income under that default is computed without Chapter VIII deductions, apart from a few listed exceptions. Section 123 sits in Chapter VIII and isn't one of them.

So the deduction helps only someone who has opted out of the default under section 202(4). The 2025 Act doesn't use the words "old regime" and "new regime", but that is what people mean by them. If you are on the default rates, an ELSS gives you no deduction at all.

The ₹1,50,000 limit also covers everything in Schedule XV together, not ELSS alone. Check your own position with a tax professional or on the Income Tax Department's site.

How does the lock-in work?

Each investment is locked for 3 years. With a lump sum that is simple. With a SIP, each instalment is treated as its own investment, so a monthly plan unlocks month by month. You can't sell during that time, whatever the market does, which means an ELSS isn't a home for money you might need.

What risks does it carry?

Full equity risk. An ELSS can lose money, and a tax saving doesn't protect against that. In S&P's SPIVA India report for mid-2026, 64% of ELSS funds lagged the S&P India BMI over 3 years, 71% over 5 years and 80% over 10 years. That says nothing about any one fund, but it is a reminder that the label alone doesn't mean a good result, and costs matter here too.

Who should look into ELSS at all?

Only someone who pays tax outside the default regime gets the deduction, and many students and first-time earners pay little or no income tax anyway. It makes little sense to choose a fund for a tax benefit you can't use. Start from whether you want an equity fund, and treat the tax rules as a detail.

How are gains taxed?

Under section 198 of the 2025 Act, long-term capital gains on equity-oriented fund units above ₹1,25,000 are taxed at 12.5%, where the securities transaction tax conditions are met. Rates change with each Budget, so check the current year.

Frequently asked questions

What is the lock-in period of ELSS?

3 years, by statute. Each SIP instalment has its own 3-year lock-in.

Can I claim ELSS under the new tax regime?

No. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, the deduction in section 123 isn't available under the default regime; you must opt out under section 202(4). Confirm your position with a tax professional.

Is ELSS risk-free?

No. It holds at least 80% in equity, so it can lose money, and you can't exit for 3 years.

ELSS kya hota hai?

ELSS ek equity mutual fund hai jisme 3 saal ka lock-in hota hai. Tax deduction tabhi milta hai jab aap default tax regime se opt out karte ho. Paisa doob bhi sakta hai.
