What is a market correction?

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

A market correction is a fall of about 10% to 20% from a recent high. It is common usage rather than an official definition. The Nifty 50 has had six since 2007, with a median depth of 15.1%. A fall of 20% or more is usually called a bear market or a crash, and there have been four.

Key takeaways

  • A correction usually means a fall of 10% to 20% from a recent high; it is a convention, not a rule set by any regulator.
  • The Nifty 50 has had 6 corrections and 4 deeper falls since 2007.
  • The median correction reached 15.1% below the high.
  • Corrections took a median of 91 sessions from peak to low.
  • The median recovery took 153 sessions from the peak.

What is a market correction?

A market correction is a fall of roughly 10% to 20% in a market or index from a recent high. The word suggests prices had run ahead of themselves and are settling back, but it describes size, not cause. No Indian regulator defines it, so people use it loosely, and some apply it to a single share as well.

How is a correction different from a crash?

Mainly in size and speed. A fall of 20% or more from a high is commonly called a bear market. Crash has no fixed meaning, but it suggests a sudden, deep fall, like March 2020, when the Nifty lost 38.4% in 47 sessions. A correction tends to be slower and shallower.

In this record, a correction took a median of 91 sessions to reach its low, nearly double the time the COVID crash took to fall more than twice as far. The trouble is that every crash starts out as a correction.

A 10% fall looks identical whichever way it is heading, and you only find out later which kind you were in. The guide to what happens after a 10% fall shows how widely things went from that point. Three of the falls that crossed 10% after 2010 went past 20%, so a correction label applied too early can be falsely reassuring. These words are useful for describing the past, and much less useful for working out what is about to happen.

What usually causes a correction?

There is rarely one clean cause. Corrections tend to follow changes in what investors expect: about interest rates, company earnings, oil prices, money moving in or out of the country, or news from abroad. SEBI's investor booklet calls this market risk: risk that comes from factors affecting the overall performance of securities and the general economy. It affects most shares at once, which is why even a well-spread portfolio falls in a correction.

How long do corrections last?

In this record, the median correction took 91 sessions to go from peak to low, and 153 sessions from the peak back to the old high, so about seven months in total. The range was wide: the 2019 fall was over within six months, while the fall that began in September 2024 took more than a year to recover. For the full table of recovery times, see the Nifty recovery guide.

Are corrections normal?

Yes. Six corrections and four deeper falls in about nineteen years works out to one every couple of years, and every investor who stays in the market for long will sit through several. That doesn't make them comfortable, and a share you own can fall much further than the index. The panic-selling guide covers what tends to go wrong during one.
