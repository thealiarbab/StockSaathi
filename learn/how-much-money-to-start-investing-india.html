How much money do you need to start investing in India?

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

There is no fixed minimum. The real floor is the price of one share or a fund's minimum instalment. On 5 November 2026 the median NSE share closed at ₹196.90, and AMFI says a SIP can start at ₹500 a month, or ₹250 under Chhoti SIP. A low price doesn't make a share cheap, though.

Key takeaways

  • The median share on NSE's main equity series closed at ₹196.90 on 5 November 2026.
  • 947 of 2,742 shares closed under ₹100, and 1,933 closed under ₹500.
  • AMFI says a SIP instalment could be as small as ₹500 a month, or ₹250 a month under Chhoti SIP.
  • The price of a share says nothing about whether it is a good or safe investment.

What does one share actually cost?

Less than most people think. The table groups every share in NSE's main equity series by its closing price on one day. StockSaathi shows counts here, not names, because a list of low-priced shares would look like a tip.

What decides the real minimum for shares?

The price of one share and your broker's charges. You can usually buy a single share, so someone with ₹500 could buy any of the shares in the first two rows of the table. Whether that is a sensible purchase is a separate question.

Is there a minimum amount to start investing?

No single minimum applies. For shares you can usually buy one at a time, so your floor is the price of that one share plus charges. For mutual funds, it is the scheme's minimum, and AMFI says a SIP instalment could be as small as ₹500 a month, and ₹250 a month under Chhoti SIP. An ETF trades like a share, so one unit is the floor there.

Is a low-priced share a bargain?

No, and this trap catches many beginners. A share at ₹20 isn't cheaper than a share at ₹2,000 in any useful sense, because the price depends on how many shares the company has. A company with a tenth as many shares would trade at about ten times the price for the same business. What matters is the price compared with what the company earns, which the P/E ratio guide explains.

If you have ₹1,000 to invest, it buys the same slice of value whether that comes as 50 shares or half a share's worth, and in practice many of the lowest-priced shares are small and thinly traded, which makes them riskier and harder to sell. Beginners tend to feel better owning 500 shares of something than 1 share of something else, but that feeling is an illusion: the count of shares you hold tells you nothing about how much of the company you own, or how it will do. It is a good habit to look past the number on the screen and ask what you would be buying.

How do charges affect small amounts?

They matter far more on a small order. Suppose a trade costs you ₹20 in charges. On a purchase of ₹500 that is 4% gone before the share has moved, and on a purchase of ₹50,000 it is 0.04%.

That ₹20 is only an example, since charges vary between brokers and products, and SEBI's booklet tells investors to note all charges and keep a record. So ask for the full list before you start.

What should come before investing?

SEBI's financial education material treats investing as one part of planning: you start by understanding your current financial situation. A sensible order is to know what you earn and spend, keep money aside for emergencies, and deal with any expensive debt, then invest what you won't need soon. The first-salary guide goes through this. Under 18, the route is different, as the student guide explains.

Can you practise with no money at all?

Yes. You can learn the whole process before risking a rupee, and a small sum in real money teaches you less than you might expect if you have no idea what you are doing with it. Practising first costs nothing.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start investing with ₹100?

Possibly. Many listed shares closed under ₹100 on 5 November 2026, but charges take a bigger bite from small orders, and low-priced shares can be riskier and harder to sell.

What is the minimum SIP amount in India?

AMFI says a SIP instalment could be as small as ₹500 a month, and ₹250 a month under Chhoti SIP. Each scheme sets its own minimum.

Is a ₹10 share better than a ₹1,000 share?

No. The price alone says nothing about value, because it depends on how many shares a company has. Compare price with earnings instead.

Investing shuru karne ke liye kitne paise chahiye?

Koi fixed minimum nahi hai. Ek share ki price ya SIP ka minimum, jo AMFI ke hisaab se ₹500 mahina ya Chhoti SIP mein ₹250 ho sakta hai. Par sasti price ka matlab sasta share nahi hota.
