The Nifty 50's biggest one-day falls

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

The biggest one-day fall in the Nifty 50 since 2007 was 13.0% on 23 March 2020, measured on closing prices, followed by 12.2% on 24 October 2008. Of the ten worst sessions, six came in 2008 and three in 2020. Days like these are rare, and they cluster in the middle of crises.

Key takeaways

  • The Nifty 50's worst day since 2007 was a fall of 13.0% on 23 March 2020.
  • The next worst was a fall of 12.2% on 24 October 2008.
  • 6 of the 10 worst days came in 2008 and 3 in 2020.
  • Falls of 3% or more happened on 81 days, or 1.73% of sessions.
  • Winning back a worst day took a median of 53 sessions, though the range was huge.

What was the biggest one-day fall in the Nifty?

On 23 March 2020 the index closed at 7,610.25, down 13.0% from 8,745.45 the session before. It came twelve days after the World Health Organization described COVID-19 as a pandemic, and it was also the lowest close of that crash. The very next day the index rose 2.5%, and it won the day's loss back in 9 sessions. The COVID crash guide follows the whole fall.

Why do the worst days cluster?

Because they come out of the same crisis. Six of the ten fall in 2008 and one in January 2009, during the global financial crisis, and three fall in March 2020. When fear is high, investors react strongly to every new piece of information, so large moves arrive close together, in both directions. In this record a terrible day was often followed by a sharp rise, as on 10 October 2008 and 22 January 2008, or by another fall, as on 21 January 2008.

The next day tells you very little. The last column shows the same thing: some worst days were won back within a few sessions, while the fall of 21 January 2008 took 645 sessions to reverse. A bad day isn't a signal in either direction. It is simply what the middle of a crisis looks like.

That is why it helps to decide what you would do before such a day arrives, and not during it. The 2008 guide and the guide to what follows a 10% fall show how differently things went from there.

How often does the Nifty fall 3% or more?

Rarely. In 4,691 sessions there were 81 days with a fall of 3% or more, about 1.73% of the time, or roughly four a year on average, although most came during crises. Falls of 5% or more happened on 22 days. Most days in the market are forgettable, which is easy to lose sight of when the worst ones get all the attention.

How do circuit breakers work?

A circuit breaker is a compulsory halt in trading when the index moves too far in a day. Under SEBI's rules, these apply at index moves of 10%, 15% and 20%, either way, whichever of the Sensex or the Nifty 50 gets there first. NSE's table sets out the halts: a move of 10% before 1 pm stops the market for 45 minutes, followed by a 15-minute pre-open call auction, with shorter halts later in the day.

A 15% move before 1 pm stops it for 1 hour 45 minutes plus the same auction, and a 20% move stops trading for the rest of the day. The pause gives everyone time to take stock.

Falls of 5% or more happened on 22 of 4,691 sessions since 2007, nearly all of them during the 2008 and 2020 crises.
