Adani and Hindenburg 2023: a share price timeline

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

A report by the short seller Hindenburg Research on 24 January 2023 was followed by a sharp fall in Adani group shares. Five of them lost between 39.2% and 76.7% by their 2023 lows. This timeline records dates and prices only. It doesn't judge the allegations, which the Supreme Court also said it wasn't deciding.

Key takeaways

  • Five Adani group shares fell between 39.2% and 76.7% from their 24 January 2023 closes to their 2023 lows.
  • Adani Enterprises' follow-on offer ran from 27 January to 31 January 2023, was fully subscribed, and was then withdrawn with money returned.
  • On 3 January 2024 the Supreme Court declined to move the investigation away from SEBI and said a third-party report can't be regarded as conclusive proof.
  • On 30 October 2026, 3 of the 5 shares were still below their 24 January 2023 close.

How did Adani shares move after January 2023?

Very differently from one another. The table measures each share from its close on 24 January 2023, the last close before the fall. Prices are adjusted by the data provider for later splits, which is why Adani Power shows a lower figure than was quoted at the time.

What happened in January 2023?

On 24 January 2023 Hindenburg Research, which the Supreme Court described as an activist short seller, published a report making allegations about the Adani group. A short seller is an investor who profits if a share price falls. By the close on 27 January, four of these five shares had lost around a fifth or more.

What happened to the follow-on offer?

A follow-on public offer, or FPO, is when a company that is already listed sells new shares to the public. Adani Enterprises says in its filing to the exchanges that its FPO was open from 27 to 31 January 2023 and was fully subscribed. Its board then decided not to proceed and withdrew the prospectus, and the application money was released to bidders.

What did the courts and SEBI do?

On 2 March 2023 the Supreme Court set up an expert committee headed by Justice Abhay Manohar Sapre, a former judge of the court, to assess the situation and the causes of the market volatility. The committee filed its report on 6 May 2023, according to the court's later judgment.

On 3 January 2024, in Vishal Tiwari v Union of India, the Supreme Court declined to transfer the investigation from SEBI. It recorded that SEBI had completed 22 of 24 investigations and asked it to finish the other two, preferably within three months. It said it hadn't interfered with the outcome of those investigations. It also said a report by a third-party organisation, without any attempt to verify its allegations, can't be regarded as conclusive proof.

On 18 September 2025 SEBI issued final orders in two matters, involving Adicorp Enterprises and Milestone Tradelinks and Rehvar Infrastructure. In both it said the allegations in the show-cause notices were not established, and it closed those proceedings without any direction. Those orders cover those specific matters, and they aren't a ruling on every allegation.

What can a beginner learn from this timeline?

Three things, none of them about whether any allegation was right. First, shares in one business group can move together and then separate: by October 2026 two of these five were far above their January 2023 close and three were below it, so "the group recovered" would be too simple. Second, a fall of this size can take from months to years to reverse, or may not reverse at all. Third, holding several companies from one group isn't the same as spreading your risk, as the concentration guide explains.

A single event can also pull very different prices in the same direction at first. It is worth comparing this with YES Bank in 2020, where the long slide came first and the headline came last.

From their 24 January 2023 closes to their 2023 lows, five group shares fell between 39.2% and 76.7%, measured on daily closes.

Did Adani shares recover?

It varied. By 30 October 2026, Adani Ports and Adani Power stood well above their 24 January 2023 close, while Adani Enterprises, Adani Green Energy and Adani Energy Solutions stood below theirs.

What did the Supreme Court decide?

On 3 January 2024 it declined to transfer the investigation from SEBI and asked SEBI to finish the two pending investigations. It didn't decide the allegations themselves.
