Mutual funds for beginners in India: how they work

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in shares, bonds and other assets. You buy units at the day's NAV, and your money rises or falls with what the fund holds. SEBI regulates mutual funds, and every fund carries a Riskometer. StockSaathi explains how they work and doesn't recommend any fund.

Key takeaways

  • A mutual fund pools investors' money and issues units at the prevailing NAV.
  • NAV is the price of one unit, calculated at the end of each trading day.
  • Every fund carries a Riskometer, with 6 levels from Low Risk to Very High Risk.
  • AMFI's file for 5 November 2026 shows 8,709 plans with a recent NAV, from 55 fund houses.
  • AMFI says SIPs can start at ₹500 a month, or ₹250 under Chhoti SIP.

What types of mutual funds exist?

There are many, but most fall into a few broad groups by what they hold. The table counts plans with a recent NAV in AMFI's daily file. A plan is one version of a scheme, so one fund can have several.

What is a mutual fund?

AMFI's investor booklet describes a mutual fund as a collective investment, where money from many investors is pooled and invested across assets such as equity and money market instruments. A fund house, or AMC, runs it, and SEBI regulates it. Instead of buying each holding yourself, you buy units, so a small amount gets you a small part of everything the fund owns.

The value of your units rises and falls with those holdings, and nothing about a mutual fund is guaranteed. That idea of pooling is the whole point.

Someone with ₹500 can't buy fifty companies directly, but they can own a sliver of a fund that does. In exchange, you pay running costs, and you hand the choice of holdings to a manager or to an index. Funds aren't bank deposits, and they don't pay a set rate of interest.

How much a fund can move depends on what it holds, so a fund full of shares behaves very differently from one full of short-term bonds. Check what is inside before you think about returns.

How does NAV work?

NAV, or net asset value, is the price of one unit. AMFI explains that it is calculated at the end of each trading day, by subtracting total liabilities from total assets and dividing by the units outstanding. SEBI's FAQ says most schemes disclose it by 11 pm that day.

You don't know your exact price when you place an order, and it depends on the fund's cut-off time. A high NAV doesn't make a fund expensive, because it only reflects how the units have grown.

What is a SIP and what is a lump sum?

A lump sum puts the whole amount in at once, and a SIP invests a fixed amount at regular intervals. AMFI says an instalment could be as small as ₹500 a month, or ₹250 under Chhoti SIP, and each scheme sets its own minimum. The SIP guide includes a real backtest on the Nifty 50.

How do you read the risk label?

SEBI requires a Riskometer, a pictorial meter with six levels: Low Risk, Low to Moderate Risk, Moderate Risk, Moderately High Risk, High Risk and Very High Risk. It tells you about the type of risk in what a fund holds. It doesn't tell you what the fund will return.

What do direct and regular plans mean?

AMFI's booklet explains that both are part of the same scheme, with the same portfolio and the same manager. Regular plans involve distributor commission, so direct plans generally have a lower expense ratio. The costs guide shows how that adds up.

How do you sell mutual fund units?

You redeem them, which means selling your units back to the fund at the NAV. Check for an exit load first, because some schemes charge a fee if you leave within a set period, and ELSS funds have a 3-year lock-in.

What do you need to start?

KYC and a bank account. Minors can hold units too: AMFI's guidelines say the minor is the sole holder, with a natural or court-appointed guardian, and there can't be joint holders.

Frequently asked questions

Are mutual funds safe?

They aren't guaranteed. The value of your units rises and falls with what the fund holds. Every fund shows a Riskometer so that you can see its risk level.

How much money do I need to start a mutual fund?

AMFI says a SIP instalment could be as small as ₹500 a month, or ₹250 under Chhoti SIP. Each scheme sets its own minimum.

Who regulates mutual funds in India?

SEBI regulates them. AMFI is the industry association.

Mutual fund kya hota hai?

Mutual fund mein bahut logon ka paisa ikattha karke shares, bonds wagairah mein lagaya jaata hai. Aap din ke NAV par units khareedte ho. Paisa upar neeche hota hai, koi guarantee nahi.
