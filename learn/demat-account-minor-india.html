Can a teenager open a demat account in India?

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 2 November 2026

Yes, but with limits. A demat account can be opened in a minor's name, and a parent or court-appointed guardian operates it until the minor turns 18. A minor cannot contract with a stock broker to buy or sell shares, so a trading account in their name can only be used to sell shares they already own.

Key takeaways

  • SEBI allows a demat account in a minor's name, operated by a guardian until the minor becomes an adult.
  • In India a person becomes an adult in law, a major, on completing the age of 18.
  • A minor cannot enter into a contract with a stock broker to buy or sell shares.
  • A trading account in a minor's name can only sell shares the minor already holds, for example from an IPO, an inheritance, a gift or a corporate action.
  • A minor cannot be a joint holder in someone else's demat account.

What can a minor do with a demat or trading account?

SEBI answers this in a short FAQ for minors. A demat account holds shares electronically; a trading account is the account with a broker that places buy and sell orders on the exchange. The table puts SEBI's answers side by side.

Can a 15-year-old buy shares in India?

Not on their own. SEBI's FAQ is direct: a minor cannot enter into a contract with a stock broker to purchase or sell any security. Shares can still end up in a minor's demat account, for example through an IPO application made on their behalf, an inheritance, a gift from family or a corporate action such as a bonus issue, and a guardian operates that account.

The only trading account SEBI describes for a minor is a sell-only one: it can be used to sell shares the minor already holds in those ways, and the natural guardian runs it until the minor becomes a major. A major is an adult in law; under the Majority Act, a person domiciled in India attains majority on completing 18 years.

What does a guardian have to do?

The guardian opens and operates the demat account in the minor's name and completes the KYC, the identity and address checks every account needs, along with the minor's own KYC. SEBI names the father as guardian, or the mother in his absence, and a court-appointed guardian if neither is available. Brokers and depository participants set their own document lists on top of this, so the guardian should check with the one they choose.

What happens to the account when the minor turns 18?

It doesn't have to be closed. SEBI says a minor's demat and trading account can continue once the minor becomes a major. The new adult has to confirm the balance in the account and complete the formalities for opening an account in their own right, after which they operate it themselves.

Frequently asked questions

Can a minor open a demat account in India?

Yes. A demat account can be opened in a minor's name and is operated by a guardian until the minor becomes a major. The guardian is the father, or the mother in his absence, or a court-appointed guardian.

Can a minor buy shares in India?

Not through a broker on their own. SEBI says a minor cannot enter into a contract with a stock broker to purchase or sell any security. Shares can still come to a minor through an IPO, inheritance, gift or corporate action, held in a demat account the guardian operates.

Can a minor be a joint holder in a parent's demat account?

No. SEBI's FAQ says a minor cannot be a joint holder in a demat account. The minor needs a demat account in their own name, operated by the guardian.
