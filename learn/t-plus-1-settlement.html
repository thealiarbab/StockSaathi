T+1 settlement in India explained, with worked examples

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 2 November 2026

T+1 settlement means a stock trade in India is completed one working day after the day it happens: the buyer receives the shares and the seller is paid on the next working day. All stocks have settled on T+1 since 27 January 2023, when India finished moving from T+2.

Key takeaways

  • Indian stock trades settle on T+1: shares and money change hands one working day after the trade day.
  • T+1 has applied to all stocks since 27 January 2023, when India completed the move from T+2.
  • Settlement took three working days (T+3) from April 2002 and two (T+2) from 1 April 2003.
  • Since 28 March 2024 an optional same-day T+0 cycle has run alongside T+1, starting with 25 stocks; in November 2024 SEBI's board was asked to widen it to the top 500 stocks by market value.

What does T+1 mean in the share market?

T is the trade day, the day your buy or sell order is executed on the exchange. T+1 is the next working day, when the trade settles: the clearing corporation delivers the shares to the buyer's demat account and the money to the seller's side. Until then the trade has happened, but the ownership change is not final. A demat account is the account that holds shares electronically.

Working days skip weekends and exchange holidays, so T+1 is not always tomorrow. The worked examples below use real dates from the NSE trading holiday list for 2026.

When does a trade settle? Worked examples

When did India move to T+1?

India moved to T+1 in steps. Trades used to take three working days to settle (T+3), which SEBI's settlement rules date to April 2002, and that was shortened to two (T+2) from 1 April 2003. A SEBI circular of 7 September 2021 let exchanges offer T+1 on any stock from 1 January 2022, with a month's public notice and a promise to keep a stock on T+1 for at least six months once moved.

Stocks were then moved over in batches. According to SEBI, T+1 was fully implemented from 27 January 2023, after which the T+2 cycle was discontinued. SEBI describes India as a pioneer for moving its whole equity market to T+1, which is why older books and videos describing T+2 are now out of date.

What is T+0 settlement?

T+0 means the trade settles on the same day. SEBI started a beta version of optional T+0, alongside T+1, from 28 March 2024 for a limited set of 25 stocks and a limited number of brokers. In November 2024 a SEBI board memorandum proposed making it available, in phases, for the top 500 stocks by market value, and a SEBI circular of 10 December 2024 enhanced its scope. It stays optional: T+1 remains the normal settlement cycle.

Why does settlement time matter to an investor?

A shorter cycle means your money and shares are tied up for less time. With T+1, cash from shares you sell is settled a day after the sale instead of two, and shares you buy reach your demat account sooner. It also means less time for something to go wrong between the trade and the settlement: SEBI's risk committee has said a shorter cycle strengthens the clearing corporations' risk management.

For a beginner it mostly explains small surprises: why shares bought this afternoon may not appear as "delivered" until the next working day, or why money from a sale before a long weekend arrives after it. If a date looks wrong, the contract note, the record of the trade your broker sends, shows the settlement number and date. Frequently asked questions

What does T+1 mean in the share market?

T is the day your trade is executed and T+1 is the next working day, when it settles: the buyer receives the shares in their demat account and the seller is paid. Weekends and exchange holidays are skipped.

When did India move from T+2 to T+1?

Exchanges could offer T+1 from 1 January 2022 and moved stocks over in phases. SEBI says T+1 was fully implemented from 27 January 2023, after which the T+2 cycle was discontinued.

Is T+0 settlement available in India?

Yes, as an optional cycle alongside T+1. It began as a beta on 28 March 2024 for 25 stocks and a limited number of brokers, and SEBI moved to widen its scope from December 2024. Most trades still settle on T+1.

T+1 settlement kya hota hai?

T matlab jis din trade hua. T+1 matlab agla working day, jab shares buyer ke demat account mein aa jaate hain aur seller ko paisa milta hai. Weekend aur exchange holidays gine nahi jaate.
