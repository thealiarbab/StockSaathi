Index funds and active funds explained

By Ali Arbab, founder of StockSaathi · Updated 7 November 2026

An index fund copies an index such as the Nifty 50, and an active fund's manager chooses holdings to try to beat one. Index funds usually cost less. In S&P's mid-2026 report, 85% of Indian large-cap active funds lagged their benchmark over 5 years. That's a track record for a group, not a promise about any fund.

Key takeaways

  • SEBI defines an index fund scheme as one that invests in securities in the same proportion as an index.
  • SEBI's base expense limit is 0.90% for index funds and ETFs, against 2.10% on the first slab of an active equity fund's assets.
  • S&P's SPIVA report found 80% of Indian large-cap funds lagged their benchmark over 3 years, 85% over 5 years and 74% over 10 years.
  • An index fund won't beat its index: its aim is to match it, minus costs.

How do index funds and active funds differ?

The difference is who decides what the fund holds. Everything else follows from that.

What is an index fund?

SEBI's Regulations define an index fund scheme as a mutual fund scheme that invests in securities in the same proportion as an index of securities. So a Nifty 50 index fund aims to hold the 50 companies in the same weights as the index. Nobody is picking shares, which is why these are called passive funds. AMFI's data on 5 November 2026 showed 706 schemes in this group with a recent NAV, and that count includes funds tracking many different indices.

What is an active fund?

An active fund has a manager who chooses what to buy and sell, trying to do better than a benchmark, the index it is measured against. That takes research, and the fund charges for it. In a good year a manager may beat the index by a wide margin, and in a bad one fall well behind it.

Do active funds beat their benchmark?

Most large-cap ones haven't, on S&P Dow Jones Indices' figures. Its SPIVA India Mid-Year 2026 report compared Indian large-cap equity funds with the S&P India LargeMidCap. Over the first half of 2026, 85% lagged the benchmark.

Over 3 years the figure was 80%, over 5 years 85% and over 10 years 74%. So over each of these periods most large-cap funds fell short, and a minority did better.

That report looks at one category, and other categories can show different numbers. Nobody can tell you in advance which funds will be in the winning group, though, and past leaders often don't stay on top. A fund's record also has to be read net of its costs, which is partly why the numbers look this way.

This doesn't prove that index funds are the right choice for everyone. It does show why the question of cost comes up so often: a manager has to do better than the market by at least the extra fee, every year, just to break even with a fund that charges less.

How much can costs matter?

More than the numbers suggest, because they repeat. Take ₹1,00,000 for 10 years at an assumed 10% a year before costs, with no further money added. At a yearly cost of 0.2% it ends at ₹2,54,697, and at 1.5% it ends at ₹2,26,098, a gap of ₹28,599.

Those are made-up rates, used only to show how the arithmetic works. The costs guide has more.

What is tracking error?

Tracking error is how far an index fund's return drifts from its index. Costs and cash held in the fund cause small gaps. Two funds following the same index can have different tracking errors, so it is worth comparing them.

How do the two differ in risk?

An index fund has the full risk of its index. If the Nifty falls 30%, a Nifty fund falls about 30%, as the record of crashes shows. An active fund adds the risk of the manager's choices, for better or worse. An index tells you little about smaller companies either, as the guide to company sizes explains.

Frequently asked questions

Is an index fund better than an active fund?

Neither is always better. Index funds usually cost less and match their index; active funds aim to beat it, and S&P's figures show most large-cap ones haven't over 3, 5 and 10 years. StockSaathi doesn't recommend either.

What is the difference between an index fund and an ETF?

Both can follow the same index. An index fund issues units at the day's NAV, while an ETF trades on the exchange during the day.

Can an index fund lose money?

Yes. It falls when its index falls, and it has no manager trying to avoid that.

Index fund aur active fund mein kya fark hai?

Index fund kisi index ko copy karta hai aur uska kharcha aam taur par kam hota hai. Active fund mein manager shares chunta hai aur index se aage nikalne ki koshish karta hai.
